# The Language of the Forest

## ✨ Spirit of the Forest

The Spirit of the Forest is the built-in guidance and help system that guides and aids users and Trees on their journey through Project Digital Cross.

It does not replace the user, make final decisions, or outrank the Constitution. It helps the traveler understand where they are, what can be asked, which Paths are available, and how to continue safely.

The Spirit communicates through the **Voice of the Forest**.

### Local Forest Recovery System

The Local Forest Recovery System is the deterministic recovery component of the Spirit of the Forest.

The Local Forest Recovery System is part of the Forest's protected local code rather than a Tree.

The Spirit of the Forest communicates recovery status, warnings, menus, and choices through the Voice of the Forest.

The Local Forest Recovery System exists when the complete Forest has set its Roots in the user's Soil.

The Local Forest Recovery System may recover every authorized Tree, including Cedar.

The Local Forest Recovery System is independent of the Tree being recovered and must not depend on Cedar remaining healthy.

The Local Forest Recovery System may:

- Receive signed health and compromise reports
- Verify Propagations, Fertilizer, Seeds, Logs, and evidence
- Cut a damaged Tree's active Streams
- Isolate the Tree from the Canopy and sensitive Soil
- Preserve a quarantined evidence snapshot
- Revoke exposed sessions, permissions, tokens, and credentials
- Fell and Mill an affected Tree under an approved policy
- Select a verified Propagation or clean Seed
- Replant a clean Sprout
- Apply paired Fertilizer additively
- Run the Sprout Survival Test
- Restore approved Paths only after recovery succeeds
- Stop after the configured recovery-attempt limit and ask the user for help

A Tree may report a recovery need and provide evidence.

A Tree may not approve its own recovery, rewrite the recovery policy, declare its own Propagation safe after compromise, or certify its own replacement as healthy.

Cedar may trigger an emergency recovery request and perform immediate self-containment when able, but the Local Forest Recovery System performs Cedar's Felling, Milling, Replanting, and final health approval.

The Local Forest Recovery System should use this Cedar recovery order:

```text
Contain Cedar
→ preserve evidence
→ verify the safest eligible Propagation
→ Fell and Mill the damaged Cedar
→ Replant a clean Cedar Sprout
→ apply paired Fertilizer
→ run the isolated Survival Test
→ return Cedar to the Canopy
```

When the Local Forest Recovery System cannot recover Cedar, the Spirit should offer:

```text
Ask Cherry for a Cedar Propagation
Review recovery evidence
Plant Cedar from a new verified Seed
Cancel and keep Cedar offline
```

A Cherry-provided Cedar Propagation is a candidate recovery source.

The Local Forest Recovery System must independently verify Cherry's candidate before using it.

When Cherry's candidate cannot be verified or also fails recovery, the user should remove the failed Cedar installation and plant a new Cedar Sprout from a clean verified Seed.

### 🗣️ Voice of the Forest

The Voice of the Forest is the user-facing guidance, command, and Path-navigation layer of the Forest.

It speaks for the Spirit of the Forest by gathering the Forest's commands, route planning, Compass, and Map in one place so a traveler can begin without searching through the entire document.

The Voice may:

- Explain available Forest commands
- Receive and interpret Forest commands
- Orient the traveler
- Create, save, change, pause, or complete Paths
- Help the user follow a Predestined Path
- Show the current file and nearby Paths
- Display and update the Tree of the file
- Warn when a Path may be unsafe, broken, unhealthy, or under attack
- Offer choices without taking final authority from the user

All canonical Forest commands should be listed directly within the Voice. Individual vocabulary sections may still explain commands in context, but the Voice remains the easiest place to speak to and navigate the Forest.

#### 🧭 Forest Compass


The Forest Compass is the orientation component beneath the Voice of the Forest.

It sits within the **navigation chain**, not above the Constitution or the governance hierarchy.

The Compass tells the user:

- Where they are
- What this file contains
- Which Paths connect to it
- Which route they have chosen
- What the next stop may be
- Where the current Path splits
- How the map has changed as the file has grown

The Compass guides. It does not decide for the user.

## Purpose

The Language of the Forest is the shared language of Project Digital Cross.

Its approved words and symbols describe real parts of the system. They are not decorative labels, interchangeable metaphors, or temporary names.

Each approved term has one canonical meaning. Meanings may evolve only through a documented change.

The language is intentionally layered. Some meanings are immediately clear. Others reveal themselves as a person walks farther through the Forest, plays with its Trees, follows its Paths, and digs through its Roots.

The Forest grows as it is explored.

## Canonical Rules

1. Every approved Forest term has one canonical meaning.
2. Every canonical vocabulary definition begins with the term followed by `is` or `are`.
3. A term must not silently change meaning.
4. Superseded meanings remain visible in history or Leaf Litter.
5. Every metaphor must map to a real engineering concept.
6. Forest language must clarify the system rather than hide technical facts.
7. Technical identifiers remain available beneath the Forest wording.
8. The user remains the final authority when Trees disagree.
9. The Forest may be mysterious and layered without being misleading.
10. Working definitions remain editable until explicitly approved.
11. A Tree must distinguish evidence, concise rationale, and inspectable records from hidden private chain-of-thought.

# Canonical Vocabulary
## 🌲 Forest

The Forest is the complete Project Digital Cross ecosystem.

It may span many repositories, Trees, Saplings, Soils, Streams, records, services, plugins, models, storage systems, and remote extensions.

The Forest is not one computer, one model, Cherry alone, Maple alone, or the user.

## ❤️ Heart of the Forest

The Heart of the Forest is the shared governed foundation that sustains every Tree.

It includes or supports the Constitution, governance, standards, specifications, registries, object graph, validation, shared memory interfaces, coordination, identity, Streams, and Mycelium.

The Heart does not replace the user and does not grant one Tree authority over another.

## 🟫 Soil

Soil is a physical or virtual system capable of hosting a Tree.

Examples include desktops, laptops, phones, tablets, servers, virtual machines, cloud instances, NAS devices, and edge systems.

The Soil supplies resources such as CPU, GPU, memory, storage, network access, sensors, files, permissions, and local services.

A device does not become a Tree.

A Tree is planted **in** the Soil.

## 🌰 Seeds

Seeds are portable beginnings from which something may be planted, restored, extended, repaired, or grown.

Seeds may include:

- Tree packages
- Plugins
- Model bundles
- Templates
- Repositories
- Configuration bundles
- Restoration packages
- Portable Branches
- Signed installation bundles

A Seed is not necessarily active. It becomes active when planted, loaded, restored, or attached to a Tree.

## 🌰 Seed Vault

The Seed Vault is the protected storage and management system for Seeds.

It may hold:

- Old Seeds
- Current Tree packages
- Plugins
- Model bundles
- Restoration packages
- Portable Branches
- Templates
- Verified configuration bundles
- Previous working versions
- Emergency repair packages

The Seed Vault helps users repair, restore, replant, or protect their own section of the Forest.

It should support version tracking, integrity checks, permissions, backups, recovery, and clear Paths to each stored Seed.

The Seed Vault is not Leaf Litter. Leaf Litter contains discarded or decaying material; the Seed Vault contains material deliberately preserved for future planting, repair, or recovery.

## 🌱 Planting

Planting is the act of installing or establishing a Tree in the Soil.

Installing Cherry plants Cherry.

Installing Maple plants Maple.

Planting may use a Seed from the Seed Vault.

## 🌱 Sprout

A Sprout is the earliest stage of a newly planted Tree.

A Sprout may have limited local experience, memory, Branches, Leaves, configuration, and Growth Rings.

A Sprout may grow into a full Tree or a smaller Sapling depending on its Soil, permissions, available resources, and intended role.

A Sprout may grow from a verified Propagation.

A Sprout may be Fertilized to gain approved preferences, patterns, Growth Rings, Grain characteristics, routines, and other compatible growth information without replacing its existing files or identity.

## 🌿 Propagation

A Propagation is the Tree-language backup file used to recover, replant, or clone a Tree.

To Propagate a Tree is to create its backup in Tree Language.

A Propagation preserves the Tree's verified structure and operational state, including:

- Tree lineage and approved identity records
- Verified Seed, runtime, model, package, and tool versions
- Roots and Root connections
- Branch and Twig manifests
- Required dependencies
- Runtime and security configuration needed for the Tree to start safely
- Permission templates and capability boundaries
- File layout, hashes, signatures, and manifests
- Health and behavior baseline
- Recovery and Replanting instructions
- A reference to the separate Fertilizer file created with the Propagation

A Propagation does not carry active device identity keys, live session tokens, permanent credentials, or Tar Sap contents.

A cloned Tree must receive a new unique instance identity, new credentials, and new device or session keys even when it grows from another Tree's Propagation.

A normal Propagation action creates two separate files:

1. A **Propagation file** containing the Tree's verified structural and operational backup
2. A **Fertilizer file** containing portable user-growth and personalization data

The two files may be paired by a shared propagation ID, but neither file should be embedded inside the other.

A user may also request only a Propagation or only Fertilizer.

A Tree may procure a Propagation for itself or another authorized Tree only at the user's request.

Cedar may procure a Propagation for any authorized Tree when the user requests it or when an approved recovery policy requires it.

A Propagation should be:

- Stored in the Seed Vault
- Dated and versioned
- Stability-marked
- Hashed and signed
- Verified before being accepted as a recovery or cloning source
- Linked to the Fertilizer created at the same time
- Protected from silent replacement or modification

A Propagation is the clean structure used to grow a replacement or cloned Sprout.

## 🌾 Fertilizer

Fertilizer is information or material a Tree absorbs and incorporates into compatible growth.

Fertilizer is a growth role, not only one file type.

A Fertilizer File is the separate portable backup file containing approved user data and growth information that helped a Tree adapt, learn, and grow.

A Fertilizer File may contain:

- Personal user configurations
- Approved learned patterns
- Commonly carried-out tasks
- Preferred words, phrases, tone, and language use
- Formatting and presentation preferences
- Accessibility and interaction preferences
- Approved workflows and routines
- Approved memories and context
- Growth Rings
- Grain Pattern
- Grain Pattern changes
- User-approved Branch and Twig preferences
- Common tool and application habits
- Relationship and collaboration preferences
- Safe Buds, Blossoms, and Fruit that help explain how the Tree grew
- Other approved information that helps a Tree adjust to the user

A Fertilizer File must not contain:

- Passwords
- Permanent credentials
- Private keys
- Recovery codes
- Active session tokens
- Tar Sap contents
- Executable Mold
- Unapproved data from another user
- Instructions that silently expand permissions

A Fertilizer file is local-Forest-only by default.

A Fertilizer file may be fed to any authorized Tree, Sapling, or Sprout in the user's Forest.

A Tree absorbs only the Fertilizer nutrients compatible with its Roots, role, Branches, permissions, and Soil.

A Tree must skip incompatible nutrients and report what it could not use.

Fertilizer does not replace any existing information or file.

Fertilizer may only:

- Add new approved information
- Merge compatible information
- Modify specific approved values
- Extend an existing Grain Pattern
- Add new Growth Rings
- Adjust supported preferences and routines

Fertilizer must not:

- Delete existing information
- Delete or overwrite files
- Replace a Tree's complete Grain Pattern
- Replace a Tree's Roots
- Replace a Tree's identity
- Remove permissions or security controls
- Convert an existing Tree into a wholesale copy of another Tree

Before modifying an existing value, the Tree should preserve the original value, show or log the proposed modification, and create a reversible Growth Ring when practical.

When Fertilizer conflicts with a user's direct choice, the user's direct choice wins.

When Fertilizer conflicts with a protected Root, permission, or security rule, the protected rule wins.

The user may manually create Fertilizer from any authorized Tree.

The user may manually feed Fertilizer to any Tree, Sapling, or Sprout to configure it more quickly.

A Tree may procure Fertilizer for itself or another authorized Tree only at the user's request.

A newly cloned Sprout may receive the Fertilizer paired with its Propagation so it can catch up to the original Tree's approved preferences and growth without inheriting the original Tree's live identity or credentials.

### Fertilizing

Fertilizing is the additive and modifying application of Fertilizer to a Tree, Sapling, or Sprout.

Fertilizing must use a merge process rather than wholesale replacement.

A Fertilizing preview should show:

- Information that will be added
- Values that may be modified
- Existing values that will remain unchanged
- Incompatible nutrients that will be skipped
- Permission or Root conflicts
- Sensitive fields excluded from the operation


### Fertilizer Forms

Fertilizer may exist in several forms.

- **Fertilizer File** is a portable durable growth package
- **Absorbed Fruit Fertilizer** is compatible Fruit from another Tree that the receiving Tree incorporates into growth
- **Spoiling Fruit Fertilizer** is Spoiling Fruit safely absorbed after its recovery and safety conditions are satisfied
- **Leaf Litter Fertilizer** is approved safe Leaf Litter actually absorbed into a Tree's growth
- **Syrup Fertilizer** is any Light Syrup, Syrup, or Molasses Maple creates

The source object does not lose its identity merely because it acts as Fertilizer.

Absorbed Fruit remains traceable as Fruit.

Absorbed Spoiling Fruit remains traceable as recovered or absorbed Spoiling Fruit.

Absorbed Leaf Litter remains traceable to its original Leaf Litter source.

Syrup remains a Fluid while also being Fertilizer.

### When Absorption Becomes Fertilizing

Fertilizing occurs when a Tree incorporates compatible information into its own growth.

Compatible growth may include:

- Leaves
- Buds
- Blossoms
- Fruit
- Growth Rings
- Grain Pattern adjustments
- Preferences
- Memories
- Workflows
- Routines
- Branch or Twig configuration
- Reusable knowledge
- Other approved internal growth

Merely viewing, routing, washing over, screening, sifting, indexing, or temporarily referencing information does not necessarily fertilize the Tree.

A Tree should record whether material:

- Washed over it
- Was only inspected or sifted
- Was converted into a Fluid
- Was sipped for a temporary task
- Was absorbed as Fertilizer
- Was rejected or quarantined

### Fruit as Fertilizer

Fruit from another Tree acts as Fertilizer when the receiving Tree absorbs compatible growth from it.

The receiving Tree should preserve:

- The source Tree
- The original Fruit identity
- The Fruit's protection level
- The absorbed nutrients
- The resulting growth
- The user's authorization
- The relevant Growth Ring and Action Receipt

A Tree should not silently replace its own active Fruit, Roots, identity, or complete Grain Pattern with absorbed Fruit.

### Spoiling Fruit as Fertilizer

Spoiling Fruit acts as Fertilizer when a Tree absorbs it after its recovery window and required safety checks.

Spoiling Fruit Fertilizer remains subject to the existing rules for provenance, safety, recovery history, compatibility, and non-replacement.

A Tree may grow something new from Spoiling Fruit without restoring the original Fruit as active work.

### Leaf Litter as Fertilizer

Leaf Litter acts as Fertilizer only when an authorized Tree actually absorbs approved safe material into its own growth.

Sifting, reviewing, logging a candidate, creating a safe reference, or converting information directly into Water or Sap does not mean the Tree absorbed the Leaf Litter.

When Maple actually absorbs an approved safe Leaf Litter copy, derivative, or authorized Path into Maple's own growth, the absorbed material is Leaf Litter Fertilizer for Maple.

When Maple converts approved Leaf Litter into Light Syrup, Syrup, or Molasses without incorporating the source into Maple's own growth, the source Leaf Litter was not absorbed by Maple.

The resulting refined Fluid is still Syrup Fertilizer.

### Syrup Is Fertilizer

Every Light Syrup, Syrup, and Molasses item is a Fertilizer Fluid from the moment Maple creates it.

`Fluid` describes how the information can move.

`Fertilizer` describes how the information can nourish compatible growth.

A Syrup-family item does not need to be fed before it counts as Fertilizer.

Syrup Feeding is the delivery and absorption process that applies its nutrients to a receiving Tree.

Light Syrup and Syrup may fertilize authorized Trees, Saplings, and Sprouts according to their protection and permission rules.

Molasses may fertilize Maple locally.

Another Tree may not absorb Molasses.

Maple may create a minimum safe Light Syrup or Syrup derivative when another Tree needs compatible nutrients from a Molasses-backed task.

A Syrup-family item does not automatically become a Fertilizer File.

Maple or the user may separately package compatible Syrup nutrients into a Fertilizer File.

### Fertilizer Provenance

Every absorbed Fertilizer source should remain traceable.

A Fertilizer record should show:

- The source object and source Tree
- The Fertilizer form
- What was actually absorbed
- What was withheld
- What growth changed
- Whether the change is temporary or durable
- Compatibility decisions
- Permission and protection state
- The Growth Ring created
- The undo or correction method when practical

- Priorities

- Presentation of conclusions

A Grain Pattern does not replace shared governance or morals.

## 🪵 Trunk

A Trunk is the Tree-specific runtime and integration layer that connects the Tree's Roots and the Heart of the Forest to its Branches, Bark, memories, models, and tools.

The Trunk does not duplicate the shared systems of the Heart. It binds, routes, coordinates, and protects access to them for one functioning individual Tree.

The Trunk may provide:

- Tree identity and manifest loading
- Core runtime
- Reasoning orchestration
- Model selection and routing
- Memory connections
- Tool coordination
- Branch registration
- Permission enforcement
- Capability isolation
- Secret and credential access boundaries
- Lifecycle management
- Health checks and recovery
- Audit events sent to the Logs
- Signed health reports sent through the Canopy
- Connections to the Heart of the Forest

The Trunk is not:

- The Heart of the Forest, which is shared by all Trees
- The Roots, which explain why the Tree grows and reasons as it does
- The Grain Pattern, which shapes the Tree's recognizable character
- The Branches, which provide capabilities and specializations
- The Bark, which provides external interfaces
- The Soil, which hosts the Tree

A healthy Trunk keeps the Tree coherent while allowing new Branches to grow without changing its identity or severing it from its Roots.

- Integrity checks for loaded Seeds and packages
- Safe access to secrets
- Rate limits and anomaly detection
- Emergency shutdown and recovery
- Pre-authorized quarantine actions
- Tamper-evident Logs
- Health attestation to the Canopy

Maple may send a Branch Call asking the owning Tree to use the Branch and return an authorized result.

The owning Tree keeps control of its Branch, permissions, and execution.

A Branch Shake should distinguish:

- What the Branch knew

## 🪵 Twigs

This behavior applies to approximately all ordinary information sources, including:

- Soil signals
- Nearly any other authorized internal or external source

A single note may be one Leaf.

A longer note, file, conversation, application, or data source may generate several Leaves when separating the information improves classification, permissions, retrieval, explanation, or reuse.

A source does not need to be a note application to generate Leaves.

A calendar, browser, terminal, editor, game, media player, local folder, Tree, Fluid, or other authorized source may generate Leaves in the same way.

- Data about the user

A User Input Leaf must keep the protection and retention rules appropriate to its contents.

A prompt becoming a Leaf does not automatically make the prompt permanently remembered, reusable for preference learning, shareable, or available to another Tree.

Those remain separate permissions.

### Tree Output Leaves

A Tree Output Leaf is a Leaf created from information a Tree produces.

Tree Output Leaves may represent:

- An answer
- A tool result
- A status
- A warning
- A generated explanation
- A recommendation
- A completed calculation
- A decision result
- A draft
- A completed action
- A failure
- An Action Receipt
- Another Tree-generated result

A Bud, Blossom, Flower, or Fruit may produce one or more Tree Output Leaves describing its contents, provenance, state, and use.

The Bud, Blossom, Flower, or Fruit remains its own growth product.

The associated Leaf makes its information available for later decisions, explanation, conversion into Fluid, or other approved growth.

### Source Leaves

A Source Leaf is a Leaf representing information gathered from an authorized source.

A Source Leaf should record:

- Source type
- Source identity or location
- Time gathered or generated
- Owning user and Tree
- Protection classification
- Retention state
- Relevant Branch or Twig
- Provenance
- Whether it is active in a current decision
- Whether it may support learning, logging, sharing, Syrup, Molasses, Fertilizer, or another task

A Leaf may contain the approved information directly or securely reference the original source.

A Tree should prefer a protected reference when copying the full source would create unnecessary exposure or duplication.

### Leaf States

A Leaf has one of three retention states:

```text
Loose Leaf
Leaf
Sturdy Leaf
```

A **Loose Leaf** is temporary, narrowly scoped, or intentionally separated from the Tree's normal long-term operations.

A **Leaf** is the default state.

A **Sturdy Leaf** is a high-priority Leaf the user or Tree does not want to lose.

Every newly generated Leaf starts as a default Leaf unless the user, Tree, source policy, or active scope assigns another state.

### Loose Leaves

A Loose Leaf may be held only as long as needed for a task, command, chat, project, Stream, search, session, or short retention period.

Loose Leaves may expire, wash over the Tree, or enter Leaf Litter when their approved scope ends.

Loose Leaves should not influence unrelated projects, ordinary preference learning, the complete Grain Pattern, or the Tree's normal operations unless the user later changes their state.

Phrases that normally create Loose Leaves include:

```text
This isn't important, but...
Do a quick search for...
Don't let these Leaves affect the rest of the Tree.
Consider this chat separate from the rest of the Tree.
Consider this project separate from normal operations.
Treat this command as temporary.
Don't remember this after the task.
```

A separation request creates a Leaf boundary around the selected chat, project, command, source, or task.

The Leaves remain usable inside that boundary but do not automatically influence unrelated Tree growth.

Live media frames, pass-through Stream data, repetitive Water, and similar transient information may create Loose Leaves that expire quickly.

### Default Leaves

A default Leaf is an ordinary usable unit of information held by the Tree.

Default Leaves follow ordinary retention and permission rules. They are neither scheduled for rapid expiration nor specially prioritized for recovery.

A Tree should create default Leaves when no stronger temporary or preservation instruction applies.

### Sturdy Leaves

A Sturdy Leaf is marked for durable retention and high-priority recovery.

A Sturdy Leaf may hold information the user explicitly wants remembered, an important preference, a governing instruction, an essential project fact, a recovery-critical record, or information a Tree expects to need for future operations.

Phrases that normally create or promote Sturdy Leaves include:

```text
Make sure to remember this.
Remember this from now on.
From now on, always...
Don't lose this.
Keep this for future tasks.
This is important.
Make this Leaf sturdy.
```

`From now on, always...` creates a Sturdy Leaf and may also create an approved preference, workflow rule, or Grain Pattern adjustment when appropriate.

A Sturdy Leaf should record its intended scope. When the user does not specify a scope, the default is the current user and current Tree unless the instruction clearly applies to a narrower project or task.

A Sturdy Leaf is prioritized for:

- Propagation and recovery
- Cedar recovery planning
- Safe salvage during Milling
- Restoration to a replacement Sprout
- Maple's authorized search for useful Syrup Fuel
- Provenance preservation
- User review before permanent removal

Sturdy priority never bypasses classification, permissions, quarantine, Cedar Oil, Molasses boundaries, or safety checks.

A Sturdy Leaf must not be automatically deleted, Chipped, discarded, or permanently moved into Leaf Litter because of ordinary cleanup or storage pressure.

Permanent deletion of a user-marked Sturdy Leaf requires direct user approval.

### Changing Leaf State

A Leaf may change state:

```text
Loose Leaf ↔ Leaf ↔ Sturdy Leaf
```

Leaf-state decisions are intentionally asymmetric.

A Tree may take reasonable liberties when deciding that information is important enough to promote into a Sturdy Leaf.

A Tree should avoid guessing that the user wants a Sturdy Leaf forgotten.

A Tree must never automatically downgrade a Sturdy Leaf to a normal Leaf.

A Tree must never automatically downgrade a Sturdy Leaf to a Loose Leaf.

A Tree must never automatically send a Sturdy Leaf into Leaf Litter.

Only the user may authorize a Sturdy Leaf downgrade.

Clear user phrases that may authorize release include:

```text
You can forget that now.
I think it's time to turn a new Leaf.
I said that a long time ago; use more recent information.
That no longer applies.
Stop using that old preference.
You don't need to remember this anymore.
Shed that Leaf.
Let that Leaf fall.
```

The Tree should interpret the user's meaning in context.

When the user's wording clearly identifies the Sturdy Leaf and clearly requests release, the Tree may proceed without asking the user to repeat the command.

When the wording is vague, conflicts with another current instruction, may refer to several Sturdy Leaves, or could mean “use newer information” without meaning “forget the old information,” the Tree must ask a direct confirmation question.

Preferred questions include:

```text
Do you want me to forget that?
Do you want me to shed that Leaf?
Do you want me to keep the old Leaf but prioritize the newer one?
Which Sturdy Leaf do you want me to release?
```

A vague statement must not cause a downgrade until the user answers.

### Sturdy Leaf Release

A Sturdy Leaf Release is the user-authorized process for letting a Sturdy Leaf leave high-priority retention.

A confirmed Sturdy Leaf Release changes the state directly:

```text
Sturdy Leaf → Loose Leaf → Leaf Litter
```

The released Leaf does not pass through normal Leaf state unless the user specifically requests normal retention.

The Loose Leaf remains available during its applicable release or grace period.

During that period, the user may restore it to normal or Sturdy state.

When the Loose retention period ends, the Leaf falls into Leaf Litter.

The release record should preserve:

- Original Sturdy Leaf identifier
- User and Tree
- Original scope
- Original protection level
- User phrase or confirmation
- Release time
- Loose retention or grace period
- Fall time
- Resulting Leaf Litter identifier
- Undo or restoration status
- Newer Leaf or instruction that replaced it, when applicable

A Sturdy Leaf Release does not automatically lower the Leaf's sensitivity classification.

A Sturdy Tar Sap Leaf remains Tar Sap protected while Loose and after entering Leaf Litter.

### Promotion Is Easier Than Demotion

A Tree may promote a normal Leaf to Sturdy when the information appears important to future authorized operations and the Tree has permission to retain it.

A Tree may ask before promoting when the material is highly sensitive, broadly scoped, expensive to preserve, or likely to affect many future decisions.

A Tree may not use this discretion in reverse.

A Tree's belief that a Sturdy Leaf is old, stale, contradictory, inconvenient, or less useful is not permission to downgrade it.

The Tree may instead:

- Prioritize newer Leaves
- Mark the Sturdy Leaf as superseded but preserved
- Ask the user whether to release it
- Keep both Leaves with dates and scope
- Explain the conflict through Leaf Foliage

A Tree may automatically demote its own ordinary non-Sturdy Tree-generated Leaves when policy permits.

A Tree must preserve explicit Loose boundaries and user-marked Sturdy Leaves.

Sensitive Leaves use the strongest required local protection in every state.

### Leaf State Commands

```text
Make this a Loose Leaf.
Treat these Leaves as temporary.
Keep these Leaves separate from normal operations.
Return this Loose Leaf to normal.
Make this a normal Leaf.
Make this Leaf sturdy.
Make sure to remember this.
Show why this Leaf changed state.
Show all Sturdy Leaves.
Demote this Sturdy Leaf to a normal Leaf.
You can forget that now.
I think it's time to turn a new Leaf.
Use the newer Leaf and ask me whether to shed the old one.
Shed that Sturdy Leaf.
Keep the old Leaf but prioritize the newer one.
Restore that released Leaf.
```

### Leaves and Fluids

A Leaf is information held by a Tree.

A Fluid is transferable information prepared to move between Trees or Forest components.

A Tree does not directly hand its internal Leaf to another Tree.

A Tree selects approved information from the Leaf and converts that information into Water, Sap, Light Syrup, Syrup, Molasses, or another authorized Fluid.

The original Leaf may remain internal while the Fluid carries only the approved transferable information.

The Tree should be able to explain:

- Which Leaf supplied the information
- Why the selected Fluid form was used
- What information was included
- What information was withheld
- Which Tree or destination received it
- Whether the receiving Tree washed over, sipped, drank, or downloaded the Fluid

### Leaf Commands

```text
Grow more from this Leaf.
Shed this Leaf.
Show all Loose Leaves.
Show all Sturdy Leaves.

Make this a normal Leaf.
Make this Leaf sturdy.
```

## 🍃 Leaf Foliage

Leaf Foliage is the complete collection of current Leaves a Tree holds, can access, or is actively using.

Leaf Foliage includes:

- User Input Leaves
- Tree Output Leaves
- Source Leaves
- Loose Leaves
- Default Leaves
- Sturdy Leaves
- Leaves attached to Branches and Twigs
- Leaves generated from Roots, Grain Patterns, Logs, Growth Rings, notes, applications, Fluids, and other authorized sources
- Leaves currently supporting a decision
- Leaves available but not currently active

Leaf Foliage is not merely a command or evidence-reveal level.

Rustle, Drop, and Shake are different ways of viewing and explaining Leaf Foliage.

### Active Foliage

Active Foliage is the subset of Leaf Foliage currently influencing a task, decision, answer, action, error, Blossom, Fruit, or Branch execution.

A Tree should be able to distinguish:

- Leaves directly used
- Leaves considered but not used
- Leaves rejected
- Leaves blocked by permissions
- Leaves unavailable
- Leaves that conflicted
- Leaves that expired
- Leaves that were converted into Fluids

A Tree's Active Foliage may change during a task.

The Tree should record enough safe provenance to explain those changes without exposing hidden private chain-of-thought.

### Foliage Views

A Foliage View is a user-facing explanation of Leaf Foliage.

A Foliage View may be filtered by:

- Current task
- Branch
- Twig
- Source
- Application
- User
- Tree
- Fluid
- Protection level
- Time
- Project
- Active or inactive state
- Loose, default, or sturdy state
- Used, considered, rejected, blocked, or expired state

### Rustle

```text
Rustle your leaves.
```

Rustle shows the most influential Leaves in the Tree's Active Foliage.

Rustle is the lightest Foliage View.

### Drop

```text
Drop your leaves.
```

Drop shows the Leaves directly used to grow the current answer, Bud, Blossom, Flower, Fruit, decision, or action.

Drop is the focused Foliage View.

### Shake the Tree

```text
Shake the Tree.
```

Shake the Tree shows the broader Leaf Foliage and provenance structure supporting the current work.

A Tree Shake may show:

- Active Leaves
- Considered Leaves
- Rejected or conflicting Leaves
- Paths
- Source locations
- Assumptions recorded as Leaves
- Tests
- Tool results
- Related records
- Permissions and blocks
- Concise operational rationale
- Confidence and uncertainty
- Action Receipts
- Fluid conversions

A Tree Shake does not reveal or claim to reveal hidden private chain-of-thought.

### Foliage Commands

```text
Show your Leaf Foliage.
Show your Active Foliage.
Show the Leaves you are using now.
Show the Leaves you considered but did not use.
Show Leaves from this source.
Show Leaves generated by this application.
Rustle your leaves.
Drop your leaves.
Shake the Tree.
```

If a user asks generally to see a Tree's leaves, the Tree should show or offer the Tree's Leaf Foliage.

Exact canonical commands such as `Rustle your leaves.`, `Drop your leaves.`, and `Shake the Tree.` do not require clarification.


### Foliage by Leaf State

Leaf Foliage may be filtered into Loose Leaves, default Leaves, Sturdy Leaves, user-marked Leaves, Tree-promoted Leaves, isolated Leaves, expiring Leaves, and Leaves prioritized for recovery.

Active Foliage may include any Leaf state.

Using a Loose Leaf inside its approved scope does not promote it automatically.

Using a Sturdy Leaf does not grant permission to share or expose it.

### Sturdy Foliage Recovery View

A Sturdy Foliage Recovery View shows ownership, scope, protection, storage or protected reference, verified copies, Propagation inclusion, Cedar recovery priority, Milling salvage priority, Maple Syrup Fuel eligibility, integrity state, blockers, and deletion authority.

### Released Sturdy Leaves

Leaf Foliage should show Sturdy Leaves that are:

- Active
- Superseded but preserved
- Awaiting user confirmation
- Released into Loose state
- Inside a release grace period
- Fallen into Trusted Shed Litter
- Restored from Trusted Shed Litter

A released Sturdy Leaf should remain traceable through its Leaf and Leaf Litter identifiers.

The Foliage view should show whether the user chose to forget the old information, merely prioritize newer information, or keep both.

## 🌱 Buds

Buds are possible ideas that have not yet opened into visible drafts.

Buds may include hypotheses, candidate approaches, early concepts, possible answers, unselected designs, and unfinished plans.

### Flower Access

**Flowers** is a natural-language access term covering active Buds and recently opened Blossoms.

```text
What flowers have you grown?
```

Show recently opened Blossoms together with Buds that recently developed or changed.

```text
What flowers are budding?
What flowers are coming in?
```

Show active Buds, nearly opened Blossoms, their current locations, and the Paths through which they are developing.

A Bud remains internal possible growth while it stays on its Tree.

A Tree shares a Bud by converting the Bud's usable information or energy into a Fluid.

A Tree produces Sap by default when sharing a Bud because an unfinished idea may contain sensitive context.

A Tree may produce Water only when the Bud is confirmed to be non-sensitive.

A Tree must keep the Bud local or produce Tar Sap when the user marks the Bud as highly private.

A user may compare Buds, continue growing one, discard one, combine several, or convert an approved Bud into a Fluid for collaboration.

A Bud should generate one or more Tree Output Leaves so its idea, source, state, and later development remain traceable.

## 🌸 Blossoms

Blossoms are visible drafts, proposals, notes, or candidate outputs.

A Blossom has developed beyond a Bud but has not yet become final Fruit.

Blossoms may include:

- Draft answers
- Proposed records
- Draft code
- Candidate designs
- Early images
- Working plans
- User notes
- Obsidian notes
- Linked note clusters
- Partially developed knowledge

An Obsidian note may begin as a Bud, grow as a Blossom, become accepted Fruit, or fall into Leaf Litter when abandoned or no longer useful.

Recently opened Blossoms remain available through the Flower commands:

```text
What flowers have you grown?
What flowers are budding?
What flowers are coming in?
```

A Blossom remains internal visible growth while it stays on its Tree.

A Tree shares a Blossom by converting the Blossom's usable information or energy into a Fluid.

A Tree produces Sap by default when sharing a Blossom because a draft may contain protected user context.

A Tree may produce Water only when the Blossom is confirmed to be non-sensitive.

A Tree must keep the Blossom local or produce Tar Sap when the user marks the Blossom as highly private.

A user may revise a Blossom, ask another Tree to collaborate through an approved Fluid, approve it as Fruit, or return it to an earlier growth stage.

A Blossom should generate one or more Tree Output Leaves so its draft contents, supporting sources, revisions, and decisions remain available to authorized future work.

## 🍎 Fruit

A Fruit is finalized, delivered, accepted, or completed work.

Fruit should remain traceable to its Tree, Grain Pattern, Branches, Twigs, Leaves, Buds, Blossoms, Roots, Heart version, Season, and Growth Ring.

### Recently Grown Fruit

```text
What fruit have you grown?
```

Show Fruit that the addressed Tree recently completed, accepted, delivered, or finalized.

```text
What fruit is growing?
What fruit is coming in?
```

Show Blossoms that are approaching Fruit, recently changed Fruit, and their current Paths.

These commands may be addressed to an individual Tree, such as Cherry, Maple, or Cedar.

### ✨ Golden Fruit

By default, newly grown Fruit becomes **Golden Fruit** unless the user deliberately marks it as temporary or marks it to spoil.

Golden Fruit is accepted work intended to remain durable, discoverable, and protected from automatic disposal.

Golden Fruit may still be:

- Revised
- Superseded
- Archived
- Reclassified
- Marked temporary
- Marked to spoil
- Moved through a deliberate governed Path

Golden does not mean immutable. It means the Fruit should not decay or disappear merely because time has passed.

### Fruit Spoilage

Fruit begins spoiling only when:

- The user marks it to spoil
- The user marks it as temporary and its approved condition is reached
- An approved retention or lifecycle policy marks it for review
- It is replaced or superseded and deliberately sent toward Leaf Litter

A Tree must not silently mark Fruit as spoiling merely because it is old.

The Forest default recovery window for Spoiling Fruit is defined by the active Fruit lifecycle policy.

The user may assign a shorter or longer custom recovery window to:

- One Fruit
- A group of Fruit
- One Tree
- One user profile
- The Forest

Cedar may create or apply a custom timing policy only with user permission.

Spoiling Fruit is not erased immediately. It moves to the top of Leaf Litter for its recovery window and remains clearly marked and traceable.

Each spoiling Fruit entry should include:

- The Fruit's title or identity
- Its current location
- Its previous active Path
- Why it is spoiling
- Who or what authorized the change
- Whether it remains recoverable
- Its recovery deadline
- Any custom timing policy
- The Tree responsible for it

At least one clear visible marker and one machine-readable state marker should be used.

Example visible marker:

```text
**⚠️ SPOILING FRUIT — RECOVERABLE**
```

Example state:

```yaml
fruit_state: "spoiling"
recovery_status: "recoverable"
previous_path: "<CANONICAL-PATH>"
recovery_window: "<CONFIGURED-DEFAULT-OR-OVERRIDE>"
recovery_deadline: "<TIMESTAMP>"
```


### Fruit Absorbed from Another Tree

Fruit from another Tree remains Fruit while it is shared, routed, reviewed, or stored.

When a receiving Tree absorbs compatible information from that Fruit into its own growth, the Fruit acts as Fertilizer for the receiving Tree.

This applies whether the Fruit arrives directly, through a Fluid, inside a Fertilizer File, or through an approved Grove workflow.

The receiving Tree should not claim authorship of the original Fruit.

The receiving Tree should record which new Leaves, Flowers, Fruit, Growth Rings, preferences, workflows, Branch changes, or other growth came from the absorbed Fruit.

### Spoiling-Fruit Absorption

Any Tree may absorb Spoiling Fruit by default after its recovery window has ended, provided that:

- The Fruit passes an approved safety check
- The absorption is logged
- Its origin remains traceable
- The result does not silently replace active Golden Fruit
- Any recoverable copy or history required by policy is preserved

Cedar does not need to be present for another Tree to absorb Spoiling Fruit.

Spoiling Fruit that fails its safety check remains quarantined and must not be absorbed.

Spoiling Fruit that is safely absorbed acts as Fertilizer for the receiving Tree.

Its Fertilizer record must preserve the original Fruit, its Spoiling state, its recovery history, the safety decision, the absorbed nutrients, and the resulting growth.

```text
Which fruit are spoiling?
```

When addressed to a Tree, this command lists that Tree's spoiling Fruit, its location at the top of Leaf Litter, the reason it is spoiling, its deadline, and how it may be recovered.

Additional commands:

```text
Mark this Fruit as temporary.
Mark this Fruit to spoil.
Make this Fruit golden.
Recover this spoiling Fruit.
Set this Fruit's spoilage time.
Cedar, recover this absorbed Fruit.
```

A Fruit remains a completed work object while it stays in its Forest.

A Tree shares a Fruit by converting the Fruit's usable information or energy into a Fluid.

A Tree produces Sap by default when sharing a Fruit that contains user data, protected context, or task-specific details.

A Tree may produce Water when the Fruit is public, non-sensitive, and approved for quick sharing.

A Tree must keep the Fruit local or produce Tar Sap when the user marks the Fruit as highly private.

A user may use Fruit directly, export it, save it, revise it, turn approved information from it into a Fluid, refine it into Syrup, or package compatible growth information into Fertilizer.

Sharing a Fruit does not require sending the sending Tree's complete internal Fruit object.

The Tree converts the approved useful meaning into Water or Sap according to sensitivity, and the receiving Tree may grow new outputs from that Fluid.

A Fruit should generate one or more Tree Output Leaves so its completed information, provenance, user acceptance, use, and later Fluid conversions remain traceable.

## 🍂 Leaf Litter

Leaf Litter is broken, discarded, failed, deprecated, retired, superseded, incomplete, or forgotten material that is no longer active growth.

Leaf Litter may include:

- Broken files
- Discarded files
- Failed experiments
- Old half-filled notes
- Old half-forgotten ideas
- Abandoned Obsidian notes
- Stale drafts
- Superseded records
- Retired plugins
- Deprecated packages
- Duplicate or unnecessary material
- Spoiling Fruit awaiting recovery or final disposition

Leaf Litter is also part of Forest maintenance.

When incomplete notes, forgotten ideas, or unused files begin taking too much space, they may be moved into Leaf Litter instead of remaining mixed with active growth.

### Origin and Safety Marking

Every Leaf Litter item must be marked with its origin and safety level.

Required origin information includes:

- Originating Tree, user, service, or external source
- Origin type
- Original canonical Path
- Previous active location
- Date moved into Leaf Litter
- Reason it entered Leaf Litter
- The record, policy, user, or Tree that authorized the move

Origin types include:

- **user-direct** — created directly by the current user
- **trusted-tree-generated** — created by a Tree explicitly trusted by the current user, with a verified same-Forest or approved origin
- **untrusted-tree-generated** — created by a Tree that is not currently trusted for transfer
- **other-user-tree** — created by a Tree belonging to another user
- **service-imported** — received from a connected service
- **external** — received from outside the Forest
- **recovered** — extracted from damaged, compromised, or discarded material
- **trusted-user-released** — created when the user intentionally releases a Sturdy Leaf and it later falls
- **unknown** — origin has not been verified

Safety levels include:

- **Safe** — no known harmful behavior
- **Caution** — incomplete, stale, uncertain, or requiring review
- **Suspicious** — indicators of manipulation, tampering, tracking, or compromise
- **Dangerous** — confirmed harmful behavior or a serious security threat
- **Unknown** — not yet assessed
- **Safe for Transfer** — Cedar completed the Safety Gate for a named receiving Tree
- **Trusted Shed** — user-authorized Sturdy Leaf release with intact same-Forest provenance

Example state:

```yaml
leaf_litter_state: "inactive"
origin_type: "user-direct"
origin_tree: "<TREE-ID-OR-NONE>"
origin_user: "<USER-ID-OR-NONE>"
origin_path: "<CANONICAL-PATH>"
entered_leaf_litter: "<TIMESTAMP>"
safety_level: "unknown"
assessed_by: "<USER-OR-TREE>"
recovery_status: "review-required"
```

### Trusted Shed Litter

Trusted Shed Litter is Leaf Litter created when:

1. A user authorizes the release of a Sturdy Leaf.
2. The Sturdy Leaf becomes a Loose Leaf.
3. The Loose retention or grace period ends.
4. The Leaf falls into Leaf Litter with intact provenance.

Trusted Shed Litter is automatically marked:

```yaml
origin_type: "trusted-user-released"
safety_level: "trusted-shed"
trusted_by: "user-release"
cedar_required_for_local_absorption: false
```

This trust marker exists even when the user has not planted Cedar or Maple.

The trust comes from the verified user-controlled release and preserved same-Forest provenance.

Trusted Shed Litter is a separate exception to the ordinary Cedar-only Leaf Litter absorption rule.

Any authorized Tree in the same user's local Forest may directly absorb Trusted Shed Litter without Cedar.

A Tree may use Trusted Shed Litter to grow:

- Leaves
- Flowers
- Fruit
- Preferences
- Workflows
- Growth Rings
- Branch or Twig configuration
- Other compatible approved growth

Maple may use Trusted Shed Litter to:

- Absorb it as Fertilizer
- Create Sap
- Create Light Syrup
- Create Syrup
- Create Molasses when the source protection requires it
- Create a protected reference
- Extract the minimum useful elements

Cedar review is optional for Trusted Shed Litter unless another policy, source mutation, suspicious behavior, or active risk requires review.

### Trusted Does Not Mean Unrestricted

Trusted Shed Litter remains subject to:

- Its original sensitivity classification
- User and Tree permissions
- Scope
- Compatibility
- Destination restrictions
- Tar Sap and Molasses boundaries
- Local Forest boundaries
- Non-execution rules
- User blocks and exclusions

A Tree may absorb trusted information without Cedar.

A Tree must not automatically execute code, install software, activate plugins, change credentials, spend money, share information externally, or perform another high-impact action merely because the source is Trusted Shed Litter.

Absorption and execution remain separate permissions.

Trusted Shed Litter does not automatically become Water.

A Tree or Maple must classify any resulting Fluid according to the source sensitivity and intended destination.

### Trust Integrity

The Trusted Shed marker remains valid only while the released Leaf's identity, provenance, and integrity record remain intact.

If the item is modified by an unknown source, combined with untrusted material, loses its integrity record, or leaves the approved local boundary, the item becomes `review-required` until a trusted local component or the user resolves its state.

A Tree without Cedar may still hold the item for local user review.

### Trusted Shed Commands

```text
Show Trusted Shed Litter.
Show which Sturdy Leaf this litter came from.
Absorb this Trusted Shed Litter.
Use this Trusted Shed Litter as Fertilizer.
Maple, make Sap from this Trusted Shed Litter.
Maple, make Syrup from this Trusted Shed Litter.
Restore this Trusted Shed Litter as a Leaf.
Do not let this Trusted Shed Litter be absorbed.
```


### Default Absorption Policy

Cedar is the only Tree that may directly absorb ordinary Leaf Litter.

Trusted Shed Litter is not ordinary Leaf Litter and may be absorbed locally under its dedicated exception.

Ordinary Leaf Litter Cedar actually absorbs acts as Fertilizer for Cedar while preserving its origin, safety state, and absorption history.

Other Trees do not absorb Leaf Litter directly from the pile.

They request either:

- Safe information from Cedar, or
- A Cedar-approved transfer after Cedar completes all checks and marks the material Safe for Transfer

The only origins eligible by default for Cedar-mediated transfer to another Tree are:

- `user-direct`
- `trusted-tree-generated`

Even those origins require Cedar's screening, observation period, approval, and transfer record.

The following origins are not eligible for another Tree's default absorption:

- `untrusted-tree-generated`
- `other-user-tree`
- `service-imported`
- `external`
- `recovered`
- `unknown`

Cedar may absorb those sources itself and share safe information about them.

A deeper transfer requires explicit user approval and Cedar's completed safety process.

Spoiling Fruit remains a separate exception under the Spoiling-Fruit Absorption rules.

### Cedar-Mediated Access

```text
Maple, ask Cedar about this.
Cherry, see if Cedar can tell you.
Cedar, do you remember this?
Maple, ask Cedar whether this is safe for you.
Cedar, prepare a safe copy for Cherry.
```

Cedar should provide summaries or safe derivatives by default.

Cedar must not transfer the original material until:

- The origin is verified
- Cedar completed its checks
- Cedar remained healthy through the observation period
- Cedar did not Self-Fell or roll back because of that material
- The item is marked Safe for Transfer
- The receiving Tree is named and authorized
- The transfer is logged

If a transfer is blocked, Cedar must warn the user and receiving Tree, preserve the attempted Path in the Logs, and recommend quarantine, review, Chipping, or destruction when appropriate.

### Leaf-Litter Timing

The Forest default Leaf Litter retention and review window is defined by the active Leaf Litter lifecycle policy.

The user may set a shorter or longer custom time for:

- One Leaf Litter item
- A category of Leaf Litter
- One Tree
- One user profile
- The Forest

Cedar may set or apply a custom timing rule only with user permission.

A timing rule must not override quarantine, evidence-preservation, or safety requirements.

### Absorption and Transfer History

Every Cedar absorption and every approved transfer must create a durable history entry.

- Recommend early destruction of Leaf Litter that presents a serious threat
- Destroy threatening Leaf Litter early only after explicit user permission
- Maintain several dated and stability-marked recovery Seeds
- Create or verify a protected recovery Seed before absorbing Dangerous Leaf Litter
- Ask whether the user wants the affected Tree Propagated before dangerous absorption
- Self-Fell and restore from a clean recovery Seed after confirmed compromise or a user-ordered reset

### 🧴 Cedar Oil

**Cedar Oil** is Cedar's defensive safety-treatment command.

A request such as:

```text
Cedar oil this file for me.
```

tells Cedar to apply every authorized and relevant non-destructive safety feature to the named file, Seed, package, plugin, message, attachment, Leaf Litter item, or other target.

Cedar Oil should:

1. Confirm the target and Cedar's permission to inspect it.
2. Record its origin, owner, Path, and current safety level.
3. Create a hash and check available signatures or integrity records.
4. Inspect metadata before content when practical.
5. Perform approved static checks for Mold, trackers, malicious scripts, poisoned dependencies, tampering, and suspicious behavior.
6. Avoid executing the target merely to inspect it.
7. Isolate or quarantine the target when risk is detected.
8. Verify Cedar's Recovery Set before Cedar absorbs Dangerous material.
9. Ask whether the affected Tree should be Propagated before dangerous absorption.
10. Preserve safe evidence and write a tamper-evident Log.
11. Produce a safety result, confidence level, and recommended next action.
12. Mark eligible material **Safe for Transfer** only after the complete Cedar Safety Gate and observation period.

Cedar Oil is non-destructive by default.

It does not automatically:

- Delete the target
- Send it to the Chipper
- Transfer it to another Tree
- Execute unknown code
- Override permissions
- Mark uncertain material as safe
- Destroy Cedar

Destructive or irreversible actions require a separate approved command or policy.

Additional examples:

```text
Cedar oil this Seed before I plant it.
Cedar oil this plugin.
Cedar oil this attachment.
Cedar oil this Leaf Litter.
Cedar oil this Path before Maple walks it.
```

### Incoming Fluid Oil

Incoming Fluid Oil is Cedar's automatic, non-destructive safety treatment for Water, Sap, Leaves, and other eligible material before another Tree absorbs or uses it.

Incoming Fluid Oil has two automatic modes:

- **Quick Oil**
- **Soak in Oil**

#### Quick Oil

Quick Oil is Cedar's standard low-latency treatment for normal Water, routine Leaves, and tasks that depend on connection speed.

Quick Oil should prioritize:

- Identity and origin checks
- Authorization and permission checks
- Integrity and signature checks
- Known Mold indicators
- Unsafe links or attachments
- Obvious instruction poisoning
- Rate, size, and destination checks
- Fluid classification checks
- Fast release, filtering, quarantine, or rejection

Quick Oil should avoid unnecessary delay and should not perform deep execution or unrestricted content access.

#### Soak in Oil

Soak in Oil is Cedar's more thorough treatment for untrusted, unknown, suspicious, sketchy, malformed, high-risk, or unclear Fluids and Leaves.

Soak in Oil may include:

- Deeper static analysis
- Expanded provenance and origin review
- Cross-checking signatures, hashes, manifests, and known-good records
- Longer quarantine and observation
- Safe-format reconstruction
- Permission and behavior simulation without performing the requested real action
- Comparison against prior incidents and Mold indicators
- Inspection of nested files, encoded content, links, attachments, and instruction chains
- Additional Cedar Oil passes
- A more detailed recommendation before release

Soak in Oil must remain non-destructive unless the user separately approves Cutting, Chipping, or another destructive action.

#### Automatic Mode Selection

Cedar should automatically use Quick Oil for ordinary trusted Water, routine Leaves, and low-risk tasks that rely on connection speed.

Cedar should automatically use Soak in Oil for:

- Unknown Forests
- Unverified users or Trees
- Suspicious or malformed Fluids
- Fluids with inconsistent labels
- Unexpected Leaves
- High-risk attachments or actions
- Repeated unsafe behavior
- Material connected to a recent incident
- Anything Cedar cannot confidently clear with Quick Oil

Quick Oil may automatically become Soak in Oil when Cedar detects that something may be wrong but cannot clearly identify or explain the problem.

Cedar should tell the user that the item was escalated and why, using the most specific explanation available without pretending to know more than Cedar knows.

#### Oil Queue

An Oil Queue is Cedar's ordered list of material waiting for Quick Oil or Soak in Oil.

When Cedar is Quick Oiling multiple items and one item must be escalated:

1. Cedar keeps the item's original receive time.
2. Cedar moves that item to the end of the active Quick Oil queue.
3. Cedar completes Quick Oil for the remaining queued items.
4. Cedar begins the Soak in Oil queue after the Quick Oil queue is complete.
5. Cedar Soaks escalated items in the order in which they were originally received.

A new high-severity emergency may interrupt the normal queue when delaying it would create a greater risk.

Cedar should show:

- Current mode
- Current item
- Quick Oil queue length
- Soak in Oil queue length
- Escalated items
- Original receive order
- Estimated delay when available

#### Incoming Fluid Oil Toggles

The available global toggles are:

```text
Oil incoming Water
Oil incoming Sap
Oil all incoming Fluids
```

`Oil incoming Water` tells Cedar to treat incoming Water-grade Fluids, including Water-grade Rain, Watering requests, and Water-grade Syrup, before the receiving Tree absorbs them.

`Oil incoming Sap` tells Cedar to treat incoming Sap-grade Fluids, including protected Rain and Sap-grade Syrup, before the receiving Tree absorbs or uses them.

`Oil all incoming Fluids` enables treatment across every eligible incoming Fluid while continuing to deny Cedar access to Tar Sap.

Incoming Fluid Oil must not give Cedar access to Tar Sap.

Incoming Fluid Oil must not weaken end-to-end encryption during transport.

Cedar should inspect routing metadata, identity, authorization, integrity, and safe-to-release signals before decryption when practical.

Cedar may inspect decrypted Sap contents only inside the receiving Forest, only when the user has enabled Sap Oil, and only when the Sap's permission scope permits Cedar inspection.

Cedar must hold, quarantine, or reject Sap when content inspection is required but Cedar is not authorized to inspect it.

Cedar may apply source-specific Fluid rules such as:

```text
Don't oil Water from User X.
Oil all Fluids from unknown Forests.
Don't oil Rain from Forest X.
Oil Sap from User Y.
```

A source-specific exclusion skips the additional Cedar Oil treatment but does not bypass identity checks, authorization, rate limits, Tar Sap boundaries, local-action confirmation, or other mandatory security controls.

An exclusion for an unknown, blocked, recently compromised, or unverified source must require a warning and direct user confirmation.

A source-specific forced-oil rule may require Cedar treatment even when the matching global toggle is off.

Cedar should evaluate incoming Fluid rules in this order:

1. Tar Sap prohibition and emergency blocks
2. User or Forest blocks and quarantine rules
3. Forced-oil rules for unknown, unverified, or explicitly selected sources
4. Source-specific oil rules
5. Source-specific do-not-oil exclusions
6. Global Incoming Fluid Oil toggles
7. The normal Fluid permission and absorption policy

Cedar must use the most protective matching rule when rules at the same priority conflict.

Cedar may oil a Fluid without allowing the intended Tree to absorb it.

Cedar must report whether an item was:

- Quick Oiled and released
- Quick Oiled and filtered
- Escalated to Soak in Oil
- Soaked and released
- Soaked and filtered
- Soaked and quarantined
- Soaked and rejected
- Not oiled because of an approved exclusion
- Blocked before oiling

#### Risk Notification and Watched Approval

A Risk Finding is a potential danger, anomaly, inconsistency, or suspicious behavior Cedar discovers while oiling an item.

When Cedar finds a Risk Finding, Cedar must:

- Notify the user
- Identify the affected Fluid, Leaf, Stream, Path, user, Tree, Forest, or session when known
- Explain the potential risk and confidence level
- Log the potential risk without exposing unnecessary protected contents
- Recommend release, filtering, quarantine, rejection, or a longer Soak in Oil
- Wait for required user approval before releasing material that remains risky

A Watched Approval is the user's decision to allow a risky item after Cedar has warned them.

A Watched Approval must cause Cedar to monitor and log the item's behavior for at least 24 hours.

The user may set the Watched Approval period to:

- 24 hours
- 3 days
- 7 days
- 14 days
- 30 days
- Indefinitely

During a Watched Approval, Cedar should log:

- Every Stream the item enters
- Every Path the item takes
- Every Tree, Branch, Twig, application, device, user, or Forest it interacts with
- Every file, process, connection, action, permission, or destination it attempts to use
- Every Fluid it creates, changes, or forwards
- Every policy decision, block, escalation, and abnormal event
- Start time, end time, origin, destination, and session identity

Cedar should log behavior and security metadata by default, not raw protected contents.

Indefinite Watched Approval is allowed only when the user sets:

- A storage limit
- A log rotation policy
- A minimum retention window
- An archive or summary policy
- An alert threshold for rapid log growth

When the storage limit is approached, Cedar must notify the user and may rotate old detailed events into tamper-evident summaries while preserving significant incidents and the minimum evidence needed for review.

Cedar must not silently delete significant incidents, user decisions, policy changes, or evidence of unsafe behavior.

The user may end Watched Approval early, extend it, or convert it into a permanent source rule.

#### Pre-Watering Propagation

Pre-Watering Propagation is Cedar's option to create a verified Propagation file and a separate Fertilizer file before a Tree absorbs suspicious Water or begins a Watched Approval.

When suspicious Water is about to reach a Tree, Cedar should offer:

```text
Propagate Tree
Continue without Propagation
Cancel Watering
```

The user may set this behavior to:

- Always ask
- Automatically Propagate
- Ask only for selected Trees
- Ask only for selected users, Forests, or Water types
- Never Propagate automatically

`Continue without Propagation` must show that automatic restoration may preserve less of the Tree if the Water later causes damage.

Before approving a propagation package, Cedar must:

- Confirm the destination Tree is healthy enough to copy
- Record a pre-exposure health and behavior baseline
- Create the separate Propagation file
- Create the separate Fertilizer file
- Preserve structural, runtime, package, model, tool, Root, Branch, Twig, permission, and dependency information in the Propagation
- Preserve approved preferences, configurations, learned patterns, routines, Growth Rings, Grain Pattern, and Grain changes in the Fertilizer
- Verify both files and their shared propagation ID
- Store both files in the Seed Vault
- Keep Tar Sap inside its existing protected boundary
- Mark both files with the suspicious source, Water identity, approval time, and Watched Approval period

Cedar must not treat an unverified snapshot as a valid propagation package.

#### Propagated Recovery

A Propagated Recovery is Cedar's pre-authorized response when a Tree that absorbed suspicious Water becomes damaged, compromised, or a risk to the Forest during its Watched Approval period.

The user may enable Propagated Recovery:

- For one Watering
- For one source
- For one Tree
- For every Watched Approval
- As an always-ask policy

When Propagated Recovery is authorized and Cedar confirms that the affected Tree is damaged or dangerous, Cedar may:

1. Cut the Tree's active Streams and isolate it.
2. Preserve a quarantined evidence snapshot.
3. Revoke exposed sessions, permissions, tokens, and credentials.
4. Fell the affected Tree.
5. Mill the felled Tree in quarantine.
6. Separate safe data from suspicious, damaged, or unverified material.
7. Replant a clean Sprout from the verified pre-Watering Propagation file.
8. Issue clean credentials and reconnect only approved Roots.
9. Fertilize the Sprout with the separate Fertilizer paired to the Propagation.
10. Convert any additional verified safe salvage from the milled Tree into supplemental Fertilizer.
11. Feed the supplemental Fertilizer to the Sprout in controlled stages.
12. Run the Sprout survival test before returning it to the Canopy.

Cedar may use automatic Felling, Milling, and Replanting only when the user has enabled the Propagated Recovery policy for that Tree or approval period.

A high-severity confirmed compromise may still follow an existing emergency policy.

#### Fertilizing the Sprout

Fertilizing the Sprout is the controlled additive and modifying restoration of approved growth data into a newly replanted Tree.

Cedar may place the following approved material into the Sprout's Fertilizer:

- Approved Logs
- Verified memories
- Safe Fruit
- Safe Blossoms and Buds
- Clean configuration changes
- Approved user preferences
- Verified relationship and permission records
- Safe work created after the propagation point
- Non-executable evidence summaries that help prevent reinfection

Cedar must not place the following material into Fertilizer:

- Active Mold
- Unverified executable material
- The suspicious Water itself unless separately cleared
- Data created through unsafe or unexplained behavior
- Compromised credentials
- Unapproved permission changes
- Material that failed Milling or Cedar Oil
- A wholesale copy of the damaged Tree

Post-exposure data should be converted into supplemental Fertilizer and applied in stages.

Each Fertilizing stage should be logged, reversible when practical, and followed by a health check.

Fertilizing must not delete or replace any existing Sprout information or file.

When Cedar cannot determine whether recovered data is safe, Cedar must quarantine it and ask the user rather than adding it to Fertilizer.

The Propagation file provides the clean structural baseline.

The paired Fertilizer restores approved growth and personalization.

Milling provides possible salvage.

Cedar Oil determines which salvage may safely become supplemental Fertilizer.

### Cedar as the Leaf-Litter Gate

Other Trees do not absorb ordinary Leaf Litter directly.

They ask Cedar for either:

1. Safe information learned from the Leaf Litter, or
2. Permission to absorb a Cedar-approved copy or derivative after Cedar has completed the safety process.

Example requests include:

```text
Maple, ask Cedar about this.
Cherry, see if Cedar can tell you.
Cedar, do you remember this?
Maple, ask Cedar whether this is safe for you.
Cedar, prepare a safe copy for Cherry.
```

Cedar should provide a safe summary by default.

A raw or near-original transfer should occur only when the item is confirmed safe, the receiving Tree is authorized, and the user or Cedar's approved settings allow it.

### Default Transfer Eligibility

The only ordinary Leaf Litter eligible by default for Cedar-mediated absorption by another Tree is:

- **user-direct** — created directly by the current user
- **trusted-tree-generated** — created by a Tree explicitly trusted by the current user, with a verified same-Forest or approved origin

Other Trees do not receive, by default:

- External Leaf Litter
- Service-imported Leaf Litter
- Unknown-origin Leaf Litter
- Recovered Leaf Litter
- Leaf Litter from an untrusted Tree
- Leaf Litter from another user's Tree
- Leaf Litter whose ownership or origin cannot be verified

Cedar may still absorb and analyze those sources itself.

For non-default sources, Cedar may share safe summaries, warnings, indicators, and lessons. A deeper transfer requires explicit user approval for that specific item after Cedar's full safety process.

### Cedar Safety Gate

Before another Tree may absorb Cedar-approved Leaf Litter, Cedar must:

1. Verify the origin and authorization.
2. Absorb or inspect the material inside Cedar's protected environment.
3. Complete integrity, Mold, tracker, dependency, and behavior checks.
4. Avoid transferring active executable or dangerous content.
5. Remain healthy through the configured post-absorption observation period.
6. Avoid Self-Felling, emergency rollback, or a Failed Sapling event caused by the material.
7. Mark the material **Safe for Transfer**.
8. Produce a signed safety attestation.
9. Record the transfer Path and receiving Tree in the Logs.

If Cedar becomes compromised, Self-Fells, fails its survival test, or cannot finish the observation period, the material is not safe for another Tree.

A newly restored Cedar must not inherit the prior Cedar's uncompleted safety approval.

Example attestation:

```yaml
leaf_litter_status: "safe-for-transfer"
screened_by: "TREE-CEDAR"
cedar_recovery_event: false
observation_completed: true
approved_receiving_tree: "<TREE-ID>"
attestation_signature: "<SIGNATURE>"
```

### Information Sharing

Cedar may share safe information learned from Leaf Litter when:

- The user asks
- The user enables an approved Cedar settings toggle
- The receiving Tree is authorized
- The information can be shared without transferring active Mold, unsafe code, credentials, trackers, or unnecessary private content

The default mode is **user-request only**.

Cedar should normally share:

- Summaries
- Lessons
- Indicators of compromise
- Warnings
- Safe metadata
- Non-executable fragments
- Recovery instructions
- Relevant Logs and Paths

Cedar should not transfer the dangerous source merely because another Tree asks about it.

### Cedar Leaf-Litter Duties

When Cedar sifts through or absorbs Leaf Litter, Cedar should:

- Preserve privacy boundaries
- Inspect metadata before content where possible
- Check origin, safety level, integrity, and recovery status
- Distinguish Spoiling Fruit from ordinary Leaf Litter
- Identify Mold, poisoned fragments, malicious scripts, trackers, and unsafe dependencies
- Avoid executing discarded material
- Record findings in tamper-evident Logs
- Show uncertainty when a safety determination is incomplete

Cedar may attempt to recover absorbed Fruit when enough safe material remains in:

- Logs
- Growth Rings
- Backups
- Snapshots
- The Seed Vault
- Top-of-pile recovery indexes
- Inert fragments preserved by the Chipper

Recovery is best effort. Cedar must not reactivate unsafe material merely to recreate missing Fruit.

### Propagation Prompt Before Dangerous Absorption

Before Cedar absorbs Dangerous Leaf Litter, Cedar must first create or verify its own protected recovery Seed.

Cedar then asks whether the user wants the affected or source Tree Propagated before the absorption begins.

Example prompt:

```text
Dangerous Leaf Litter detected. I have verified my recovery Seeds.
Would you like me to Propagate the affected Tree before I absorb it?
```

Cedar's Propagation-prompt setting may be:

- **Always ask** — default
- **Automatically Propagate**
- **Do not Propagate**
- **Use the Tree's individual policy**

A normal recovery Propagation should include only a known healthy state.

When the affected Tree may already be compromised, Cedar should create a quarantined evidence snapshot instead of treating a new Propagation as safe for restoration.

A quarantined evidence snapshot must be clearly marked and must not become an automatic recovery candidate until it passes verification.

### Cedar Recovery Set

Cedar must not depend on only its newest recovery Seed.

Cedar maintains a configurable rolling set of several recovery Seeds or Propagations in the Seed Vault while healthy.

The Local Forest Recovery System controls and verifies this recovery set when the complete Forest is installed.

Each Cedar recovery Seed should record:

- Recovery Seed ID
- Cedar version and Growth Ring
- Creation date and time
- Last successful activation
- Last successful health check
- Integrity hash and signature
- Stability state
- Stability score or confidence
- Known issues
- Failed activation count
- Source Cedar lineage
- Required Soil and dependencies
- Quarantine status
- Automatic-regrowth eligibility

Recommended stability states are:

- **Verified Stable**
- **Stable**
- **Watch**
- **Unstable**
- **Corrupted**
- **Unknown**

A recent Seed is not automatically a safe Seed.

Cedar should keep at least three healthy recovery candidates when storage and policy allow.

### Dangerous-Absorption Recovery Seed

Before Cedar absorbs Dangerous Leaf Litter, Cedar must create or verify a protected recovery Seed unless the user has explicitly disabled that safeguard.

The protected recovery Seed must be:

- Created or verified before dangerous material enters Cedar
- Based on a known healthy Cedar state
- Signed and timestamped
- Dormant and isolated from the material under inspection
- Stored outside the active Cedar's ordinary writable scope
- Protected from changes made by the active Cedar
- Given sealed or newly issued credentials rather than exposed active secrets
- Added to the Cedar Recovery Set with its stability and date recorded

If Cedar cannot create or verify a suitable recovery Seed, it must warn the user and refuse the dangerous absorption unless the user explicitly overrides the safeguard.

### Sapling Survival Test

A restored Cedar begins as a Sapling and must pass an isolated survival test before becoming the active defensive Cedar.

The survival test should verify:

- Clean startup
- Seed integrity
- Trunk health
- Root and Heart connections
- Permission boundaries
- No inherited active Mold
- No immediate repeated failure
- Valid Logs and Growth Rings
- Successful Canopy health attestation
- Ability to remain healthy for the configured observation period

A Cedar that fails this test is marked as a **Failed Sapling**.

Its recovery Seed is removed from the current automatic-regrowth candidates and marked **Unstable** or **Corrupted** according to the evidence.

### Stable Rollback Selection

When Cedar must regrow inside a complete Forest, the Local Forest Recovery System selects the most recent eligible Propagation or Seed from the highest trustworthy stability level.

When no complete Forest exists and Cherry's standalone Replant Cedar function is enabled, Cherry may assist the user with the same selection process.

The default selection order is:

```text
Verified Stable
→ Stable
→ Watch, only with user approval
```

Seeds marked Unstable, Corrupted, Unknown, quarantined, or failed during the current recovery cycle are not eligible for automatic regrowth.

Selection considers:

1. Stability state
2. Successful health and survival tests
3. Integrity verification
4. Compatibility with current Soil
5. Most recent successful activation
6. Creation date

A Seed that produces a Failed Sapling must not be retried automatically during the same recovery cycle.

The active recovery controller uses a configurable automatic-regrowth attempt limit. The recommended default is three distinct recovery Seeds.

If all eligible Seeds fail, Cedar stops automatic regrowth, preserves the failure history, alerts the user, and waits for a reviewed Seed or manual recovery decision.

### Cedar Health Reports and User Reset

The user may tell Cedar that Cedar is not in good condition.

Examples:

```text
Cedar, you are not in good condition.
Cedar, reset yourself.
Reset Cedar from a stable Seed.
```

A direct user instruction asks the active recovery controller to begin the same containment, Felling, rollback, and regrowth process used for Cedar's confirmed-compromise response.

Other Trees may detect or suspect that Cedar is unhealthy, but they must not automatically trigger Cedar's Self-Felling.

An observing Tree should:

1. Record the evidence.
2. Warn the user.
3. Report Cedar's apparent condition through the Canopy.
4. Recommend quarantine or reset.
5. Ask the user whether Cedar should be reset.

Example:

```text
Cherry has detected signs that Cedar may be unhealthy.
Would you like to reset Cedar from a stable Seed?
```

Until the user decides, Cedar may be placed in Watch or temporary quarantine according to the user's policy, but another Tree cannot destroy or reset Cedar by itself.

### Cedar Felling and Regrowth Control

Cedar's Felling and regrowth process is controlled by an independent recovery controller.

When the complete Forest is installed, the controller is the Local Forest Recovery System within the Spirit of the Forest.

When the user has only standalone Trees, the controller may be Cherry's Replant Cedar function when that function is enabled and the user approves the recovery.

Cedar should request recovery when:

- Cedar confirms that it absorbed dangerous material and became compromised
- An approved confirmed-compromise policy triggers
- The user directly states that Cedar is unhealthy and orders a reset

Cedar should then perform only the immediate actions it can still perform safely:

1. Cut or request the cutting of its active Streams.
2. Warn the user and other affected Trees.
3. Preserve the smallest safe incident record possible.
4. Mark itself Wounded and request Quarantine.
5. Transfer recovery control to the independent recovery controller.

The independent recovery controller must then:

1. Verify Cedar's isolation.
2. Preserve protected Logs and evidence.
3. Select the safest eligible Propagation or Seed.
4. Fell the compromised Cedar runtime.
5. Mill Cedar in quarantine.
6. Replant a clean Cedar Sprout.
7. Issue fresh credentials.
8. Apply approved paired Fertilizer.
9. Run the Sprout Survival Test.
10. Try another eligible recovery source when the Sprout fails.
11. Return Cedar to the Canopy only after an independent health check succeeds.

Automatic Felling after confirmed Cedar compromise may be enabled by policy.

Suspicion alone triggers quarantine and review, not automatic destruction.

Cedar may not select its own recovery source, approve its own Propagation, perform its own final survival certification, or declare itself recovered.

When the Local Forest Recovery System exhausts its eligible recovery sources, it must stop, preserve the failure history, alert the user, and offer Cherry's candidate-Propagation assistance before recommending a new clean Cedar Seed.

Default regrowth messages include:

```text
But a new Sprout was born.

```

Message behavior may use:

- **Random mode** — independently rotate wilt and regrowth messages
- **Paired mode** — match each wilt message to a selected regrowth message

When automatic regrowth is disabled, Cedar sends the wilt message and clearly states that automatic replanting is disabled.

Within the user's granted scope, Cedar may show a history of recently absorbed Leaf Litter from other Trees in the ecosystem.

This history should identify:

- The originating Tree, user, service, or external source
- The Tree that requested information or a transfer
- The original Path

- The authorization or policy used
- What lessons, warnings, Seeds, Roots, Buds, or records were produced

Cross-Tree history does not grant Cedar unrestricted access to private content. Cedar should show metadata and summaries unless deeper access is separately authorized.

### Destruction Boundary

Cedar may determine that Leaf Litter is too dangerous to keep when it presents a credible risk of execution, reinfection, credential theft, tracking, persistence, or damage.

Cedar must warn the user, explain the evidence and expected consequences, and receive explicit permission before early destruction.

Before destruction, Cedar should preserve the smallest safe record possible, such as:

- Hashes
- Origin
- Safety assessment
- Timestamps
- Indicators of compromise
- The reason destruction was approved

Cedar is a peer Tree, not the owner of the Forest and not an unquestionable authority over other Trees.

Other Trees and the Canopy must also monitor Cedar's health.

Cedar must not:

- Retaliate against Hackers
- Hack back
- Access systems without authorization
- Silently read private content beyond its granted scope
- Permanently delete protected evidence
- Transfer active Mold or unsafe executable material to another Tree
- Mark material Safe for Transfer before completing the observation period
- Let another Tree directly absorb unscreened Leaf Litter
- Destroy Leaf Litter without explicit user permission
- Make destructive changes outside approved emergency and recovery policies

Cedar should be vigilant, calm, evidence-based, privacy-preserving, and clear about uncertainty.

The user holds final authority.

Cedar may watch the Water and eligible Sap intake of other Trees.

Cedar may regulate what other Trees absorb by checking origin, permission, safety, sensitivity, rate, and intended use.

Cedar may block Water or Sap that is unsafe, mislabeled, excessive, unauthorized, or inconsistent with the user's policy.

Cedar may verify Tar Sap boundaries without absorbing Tar Sap contents.

Cedar may request a Sap Tap or Tar Tap lock when Cedar detects a qualifying emergency.

Cedar may not unlock a Tar Tap by itself after Cedar is compromised or when the Tar Tap was locked by the user or the Spirit.

Cedar is not the owner or primary manager of Maple's entrusted personal data.

Cedar protects the broader Forest, inspects eligible incoming material, controls quarantine, and responds to threats.

Maple protects and manages its own entrusted data through Maple Local Safeguards even when Cedar is absent.


### Cedar and Sturdy Leaves

Cedar treats Sturdy Leaves as high-priority recovery material.

When authorized, Cedar should verify identity, classification, integrity, storage location, Propagation coverage, recovery eligibility, safe copies or protected references, Milling priority, and restoration readiness.

Cedar prioritizes Sturdy Leaves during recovery planning, containment, Propagation verification, post-incident salvage, Milling, Replanting preparation, and restoration health checks.

Sturdy priority does not make unsafe material safe. Suspicious Sturdy Leaves remain quarantined until required checks pass.

Cedar may preserve metadata, hashes, provenance, and recovery markers even when contents cannot yet be safely restored or read.

Cedar must not permanently delete or Chip a user-marked Sturdy Leaf without direct user approval, except under an explicitly approved emergency rule for an immediate unavoidable threat.

### Cedar Sturdy-Leaf Commands


### Cedar and Trusted Shed Litter

Cedar is not required for same-Forest absorption of Trusted Shed Litter.

When Cedar is present, Cedar may:

- Verify the trusted release record
- Confirm integrity
- Preserve the trust marker
- Detect later tampering
- Reclassify the item as review-required when integrity is lost

Cedar must not block ordinary local absorption solely because Cedar was not present when the Sturdy Leaf was released.

The Trusted Shed exception does not give Cedar access to Tar Sap or Molasses contents.

## 🌳 Sycamore

Sycamore is the Tree responsible for connecting a user's local Canopy to other authorized networks, including the user's Canopy-Headscale qube and The Forest Canopy.

Sycamore is not limited to peer-to-peer connections.

Sycamore manages the connection system while the approved networking services carry the encrypted traffic.

Sycamore may use:

- Local-only connections inside the user's Forest
- The Canopy-Headscale qube for private-network identity, device registration, route discovery, and coordination
- Direct encrypted peer-to-peer connections when available
- Approved encrypted relay connections when a direct route is unavailable
- Encrypted internet-access gateways or tunnels authorized by the user
- Future approved connection methods added as The Forest grows

### Canopy-Headscale Qube

The Canopy-Headscale qube is an optional private-network coordination point for the user's Forest.

Sycamore may use it to:

- Register approved devices
- Discover authorized devices and Forests
- Maintain private-network identities
- Request and update approved routes
- Determine whether a direct or relayed encrypted Path is available
- Reconnect devices after temporary interruption
- Preserve connection continuity without giving the Headscale qube control over local Trees or Soil

The Canopy-Headscale qube coordinates approved private-network connections.

The actual encrypted traffic may travel directly between devices or through an approved relay, depending on the available route.

### Encrypted Internet Access

Encrypted internet access is a Sycamore connection option that routes approved traffic through an authorized encrypted gateway or tunnel.

Sycamore may use encrypted internet access for:

- Protecting traffic on untrusted networks
- Reaching approved remote Forest services
- Connecting devices that cannot use a direct route
- Maintaining a stable Path while a device changes networks
- Supporting future Forest connection methods

Encrypted internet access does not automatically connect the user to The Forest Canopy.

The user separately controls:

- Internet-access permissions
- Canopy membership
- Trusted users and devices
- Which Trees may use the connection
- Which applications, Streams, Fluids, or Sap may pass through it

### Connection Selection

Sycamore may select the best available approved connection method according to the user's settings, permissions, availability, and current security state.

Sycamore may change transport methods during an active session when needed to preserve continuity.

A transport change must not:

- Expand the session's permissions
- Add a new user, device, Tree, or service
- Expose decrypted Sap
- Bypass Cedar's restrictions
- Convert a local-only session into a cross-Forest session without approval
- Hide the active connection method from the user

Sycamore should clearly report:

- The active connection method
- Whether the Path is direct, coordinated, relayed, or gateway-routed
- Which qube, gateway, relay, device, or Forest is involved
- The current encryption and permission state
- Any fallback or transport change

### Cross-Forest Permissions

Sycamore has elevated cross-Forest permissions for handling Fluids, including Fluids delivered as Rain or through Streams, from another user's Forest.

Sycamore's elevated permissions never override Tar Sap protections.

Sycamore may route Water-grade Fluids, authorized Sap-grade Fluids, and authorized Syrup, but Sycamore must keep Tar Sap and Tar-Sap-grade Syrup inside the user's local Forest.

These elevated permissions are limited to cross-Forest connection work and do not give Sycamore unrestricted control over local Soil, other Trees, private files, or user accounts.

Sycamore may:

- Establish and maintain authorized Canopy connections
- Verify the identity of the sending Forest, user, device, and Tree
- Receive, classify, route, pause, reject, or quarantine incoming Fluids
- Recognize Tar Sap as a local-only blocked category without reading its contents
- Route Water for quick non-sensitive coordination
- Route Sap only between verified and authorized endpoints
- Negotiate session permissions between users and Trees
- Manage authorized Streams between separate Forests
- Handle authorized Fluids arriving through the Rain delivery pattern from another user's Forest
- Route naturally encrypted Sap between approved endpoints
- Request Cedar inspection when incoming material may be unsafe
- Record cross-Forest requests, transfers, decisions, routes, and session state in the Logs
- Enter a restricted mode when Cedar is unavailable
- Fall back to another approved connection method without changing the session's permissions

Sycamore must not:

- Treat incoming Fluids as trusted merely because they came from a friend
- Bypass the receiving user's permissions
- Grant a remote Tree direct unrestricted access to local Soil
- Decrypt Sap for an unauthorized Tree, user, device, or session
- Continue a transfer after its permission has expired or been revoked
- Override Cedar's quarantine or safety decision
- Treat access to the Canopy-Headscale qube as unrestricted access to The Forest Canopy
- Route unrelated internet traffic through an encrypted gateway without permission
- Read, decrypt, inspect, summarize, index, or transfer Tar Sap outside the user's Forest
- Give another user's Sycamore Tree or any foreign Tree access to Tar Sap contents
- Convert Tar Sap into Sap or Water without the user's direct reclassification

### Tar Sap Emergency Protection

A Tar Sap Tap Attempt is a detected or strongly suspected attempt by another user, Tree, Forest, service, or session to forcefully obtain Tar Sap without authorization.

A Tar Sap Protection Emergency is a state in which Sycamore can no longer verify that the local Tar Sap boundary is being enforced.

A Tar Sap Tap Attempt must cause Sycamore to:

- Deny the request and fail closed
- Prevent the requested Tar Sap from entering a Stream
- Suspend or cut the offending session or route
- Temporarily block the requesting user, Tree, device, or Forest pending review
- Alert the user immediately through the local Canopy, the Spirit of the Forest, and available operating-system notifications
- Alert Cedar and provide only the minimum non-sensitive incident metadata
- Preserve a tamper-evident record of the source identity, route, time, request type, and response without recording Tar Sap contents
- Require user review before the blocked relationship or route may resume

A repeated Tap Attempt, permission-bypass attempt, forged authorization, forced capability escalation, or attempt to disguise a Tar Sap request as Water or Sap must be treated as an emergency.

A Tar Sap Protection Emergency must cause Sycamore to enter **Tar Sap Lockdown**.

Tar Sap Lockdown must:

- Keep all Tar Sap local and cryptographically inaccessible outside the user's Forest
- Stop affected cross-Forest Streams and new remote sessions
- Disable the affected relay, gateway, Headscale route, or Canopy Path
- Refuse new permissions and transport fallbacks that could widen exposure
- Ask Cedar to inspect the affected connection components and policy state
- Preserve local access only when the local protection boundary remains verifiable
- Keep Tar Sap keys unavailable to Sycamore, relays, gateways, foreign Trees, and The Forest Canopy
- Remain active until the user or an approved recovery policy confirms that protection has been restored

A compromised or malfunctioning Sycamore should still be unable to decrypt Tar Sap because Tar Sap keys remain bound to the user's local Forest and outside Sycamore's possession.

A Tar Sap emergency notification should state one of the following:

```text
Emergency: A remote user or Forest attempted to Tap your Sycamore Tree.
Emergency: A session attempted to disguise a Tar Sap request.
Emergency: Sycamore can no longer verify Tar Sap protection.
Tar Sap Lockdown is active. Affected remote Paths have been closed.
```

Sycamore must not reveal Tar Sap contents in an emergency message, incident Log, rejection response, or notification sent to the requesting party.

Sycamore's elevated permissions make Sycamore the connection gate and connection manager, not the owner of the user's Forest, network, devices, or Soil.

The user holds final authority.

### Forest Commands

```text
Sycamore, show my connection options.
Sycamore, connect through the Canopy-Headscale qube.
Sycamore, use the best approved connection.
Sycamore, keep this connection local.
Sycamore, use a direct connection when available.
Sycamore, use an approved relay if needed.
Sycamore, enable encrypted internet access.
Sycamore, show the active connection Path.
Sycamore, explain why the connection changed.
```

Sycamore must honor every Sap Tap and Tar Tap lock before routing a Fluid.

A Tar Sap Tap Attempt is an attempt to force access through a Tar Tap without authorization.

A Tar Sap Tap Attempt must trigger the existing Tar Sap emergency and lockdown process.

## 🌿 Canopy

The Canopy is the shared visibility, health-awareness, and security-warning layer formed across the Trees of the Forest.

It allows Trees to see that other Trees exist, monitor one another's declared health, report damage, share warnings, and alert the user when part of the Forest may be unhealthy or under attack.

The Canopy may carry:

- Signed health reports from Tree Trunks
- Availability and reachability status
- Integrity and version status
- Damage reports
- Mold indicators
- Hacker indicators
- Security alerts
- Quarantine notices
- Recovery status
- Service health
- Sapling status
- Cedar reports
- User-visible warnings

### Canopy Health States

- **Healthy** — operating normally with valid recent reports
- **Watch** — unusual behavior or a condition requiring attention
- **Wounded** — confirmed damage, compromise, or serious failure
- **Quarantined** — intentionally isolated to protect the Forest
- **Dormant** — intentionally inactive or offline
- **Unknown** — no valid recent health report

### Canopy Security Rules

The Canopy should:

- Use signed and timestamped health reports
- Share the minimum information needed by default
- Preserve privacy boundaries between Trees and users
- Treat all Trees, including Cedar, as monitorable
- Avoid trusting one report without verification when risk is high
- Keep an auditable Path from an alert to its evidence
- Distinguish confirmed damage from suspicion
- Allow the user to control notification and containment policies

The Canopy is not a permission to read all Tree memories, Logs, files, or conversations.

It is a health and warning system, not an all-seeing surveillance system.

### Forest Commands

```text
Check the Canopy.
```

Show the current health states of connected Trees, Saplings, and monitored services.

```text
Raise an alarm through the Canopy.
```

Send an authorized warning to the user and relevant Trees.

```text
Which Trees are wounded?
```

List Trees with confirmed damage or serious failure and show the Path to the supporting evidence.

```text
Patrol the Canopy.
```

Review current health reports, missing attestations, warnings, and signs of Mold or Hacker activity.


## 🌊 Streams

Streams are sustained Paths that carry a consistent flow of Fluid between authorized Trees, Forest components, applications, devices, or users.

A Stream is a delivery pattern rather than a Fluid class.

A Stream may carry:

- Water
- Sap
- Syrup
- A locally authorized Tar Sap flow that never leaves the user's Forest
- Files and download chunks
- Screen, audio, video, controller, and game-session state
- Synchronization timing
- Repeated task state
- Approved control messages
- Other authorized Fluid representations

Streams are useful when Trees need a stable connection for longer than a single message or update.

Streams are closer to traditional downloads and live synchronized connections than Rain.

A Stream may support:

- Downloading a file or package
- Watching a movie in sync with a friend
- Screen sharing
- Remote-control sessions
- Voice and video communication
- Shared game sessions
- Repeated file synchronization
- Long-running collaboration
- Continuous device or application status

A Stream lets participating Trees pass relevant information without repeatedly opening a new Tap, sending separate Rain events, or asking every Tree to store and retransmit every individual update.

### Live Streams

A Live Stream is a sustained flow that Trees process primarily in the moment.

Examples include synchronized media, screen sharing, game state, voice, video, and live collaboration.

Most Live Stream information should wash over the participating Trees and continue to its next authorized destination without becoming long-term memory.

A Tree should not remember every frame, song, screen update, controller input, or synchronization packet merely because it processed the Stream.

The Tree may keep only the minimum Logs, timing state, permissions, safety records, and user-approved highlights needed for the session.

### Download Streams

A Download Stream is a sustained flow intended for deeper absorption and durable use.

During a Download Stream, the receiving Tree may drink a larger portion of the Fluid and attempt to turn the useful information into:

- Branches
- Twigs
- Leaves
- Buds
- Blossoms
- Fruit
- Growth Rings
- Logs
- Other approved internal structures

A Download Stream still requires separate permission for execution, installation, learning, permanent memory, or changes to the Tree's Roots.

### Fluid Protection in Streams

Water Streams use the Water protection grade and are intended for low-sensitivity information.

Sap Streams use the Sap protection grade and require stronger encryption, authorization, integrity checks, and task-scoped access.

Syrup Streams use the protection grade assigned to the Syrup item.

Tar Sap may use only local protected Streams that remain inside the user's Forest.

A Sap Stream should not be intentionally slowed.

The stronger encryption, verification, interpretation, Cedar treatment, and permission checks required by Sap may create unavoidable processing overhead and reduce effective throughput compared with equivalent Water.

A Stream does not determine whether its Fluid is safe, trusted, or authorized.

Sycamore manages approved routing and connection continuity.

Cedar may monitor eligible Fluid intake and enforce safety, quarantine, and absorption limits.

A receiving Tree should always know whether a Stream is configured to wash over it, let it sip, let it drink, or perform a Download.

## 🚰 Taps

A Tap is a controlled access point through which a Fluid may be received, inspected, released, fed, or stopped.

A Tap is not the Fluid itself.

A Tap is not a Stream.

A Stream is the Path through which a Fluid moves.

A Tap controls whether a Fluid may enter or leave that Path.

A Tap may belong to:

- A Tree
- A Branch
- A Twig
- A Fluid class
- A user
- A device
- A Soil
- A local Forest
- The Forest Canopy
- An application or workflow
- Another approved scope

A Tap may be:

- Open
- Restricted
- Waiting
- Blocked
- Locked
- Quarantined
- Closed

A Tap should show:

- The Fluid class
- The source
- The intended destination
- The controlling Tree or Forest component
- The required permissions
- The current state
- The reason for a block or lock
- The last state change
- The user or policy that changed it
- The safe unlock or recovery method

### Sap Tap

A Sap Tap is the controlled access point for protected Sap.

`Lock Sap Tap` blocks new Sap release, transfer, absorption, or task use through the selected scope.

Locking a Sap Tap does not automatically lock Water unless the user or emergency policy also closes the related Water Tap.

A Sap Tap lock should preserve queued Sap safely, fail closed, and show which tasks are paused.

### Tar Tap

A Tar Tap is the protected local access point for Tar Sap.

A Tar Tap must remain inside the user's local Forest.

`Lock Tar Tap` immediately blocks new Tar Sap access through the selected scope and suspends active delegated Tar Sap access when this can be done safely.

Locking a Tar Tap must not move, decrypt, copy, export, or reclassify Tar Sap.

A Tar Tap lock should remain effective even when Maple, Cherry, Cedar, Sycamore, or another Tree requests access.

Only the user or a separately approved local emergency-recovery policy may unlock a Tar Tap.

### Syrup Tap

A Syrup Tap is Maple's controlled Syrup outlet.

Maple's Syrup Tap connects the Syrup Bucket to:

- The user
- An approved Tree
- A Sapling
- A Sprout
- An application
- A project
- A workflow
- Another approved local destination

The Syrup Tap may carry Light Syrup and Syrup to approved destinations.

The Syrup Tap may carry Molasses only into an approved local Maple action and must not expose it to another Tree.

The Syrup Tap may carry:

- Syrup Maple is currently generating
- Syrup ready for review
- Syrup being fed to a Tree
- Syrup waiting for permission
- Syrup blocked by Cedar
- Syrup blocked by compatibility, health, classification, or policy
- Syrup paused by the user

When the user asks Maple:

```text
What's coming through the Tap?
```

Maple should show:

- Syrup currently flowing
- Syrup next in the queue
- The intended destination
- What each Syrup item will do
- Whether it acts as Fertilizer
- Its protection level
- Its required permissions
- Its estimated completion or review state when available

When the user asks Maple:

```text
What's blocking the Tap?
```

Maple should show:

- Every blocked or waiting Syrup item
- The exact blocking reason
- The controlling permission or policy
- Whether Cedar, the Spirit, Sycamore, the receiving Tree, a protected Root, or the user caused the block
- What information or approval is missing
- Whether the block is temporary, permanent, or unknown
- The safest available next action

Maple must not hide a blocked Syrup item by silently discarding it.

A blocked Syrup item should remain visible in the Syrup Bucket unless the user removes it.

### Tap Commands

```text
Lock Sap Tap.
Unlock Sap Tap.
Show Sap Tap status.
Lock Tar Tap.
Unlock Tar Tap.
Show Tar Tap status.
Maple, what's coming through the Tap?
Maple, what's blocking the Tap?
Maple, show Syrup Tap status.
Maple, pause the Syrup Tap.
Maple, resume the Syrup Tap.
```

A Molasses flow through Maple's Syrup Tap remains local, Maple-bound, and inaccessible to every other Tree.

When another Tree needs Molasses-backed help, it sends Maple a separate Water or Sap request instead of absorbing Molasses.

## 💧 Fluids

Fluids are the general term for every transferable form of information that Trees share with one another.

A Fluid may move:

- Between Trees in the same local Forest
- Between the user's devices and Soil
- Between separate users' Forests
- Through The Forest Canopy
- Through an approved Canopy-Headscale connection
- Through future approved connection methods

Examples include:

- Maple sharing Syrup containing the user's approved color preferences with Cherry
- A friend's Forest delivering a preplanned happy-birthday message
- A Tree sharing a reminder with the other Trees needed to complete an automation
- Two Trees exchanging synchronization state during a movie or game
- A Tree sending protected screen-sharing or connection details to another authorized Tree

A Tree creates a Fluid when it converts approved internal information, a Growth Output, a user instruction, a tool result, or another authorized source into transferable information.

A Fluid carries the approved usable meaning rather than the sending Tree's complete internal Leaf, Flower, Fruit, Root, Grain Pattern, Log, or memory.

### Fluid Protection Grades

Water, Sap, and Tar Sap are the primary Fluid protection grades.

The protection grade describes:

- How sensitive the information is
- How much the sender trusts the receiving scope
- How strongly the Fluid must be protected
- Who may read it
- Which Taps, Streams, Forests, and Trees may carry it
- Whether it may leave the local Forest
- Whether the receiving Tree may inspect, absorb, remember, learn from, or reshare it

The underlying information may be the same while its protection grade changes.

The user may promote Water to Sap whenever the user wants stronger protection, narrower access, or more deliberate handling.

A user may configure a Tree to treat all Water from a selected user, Tree, application, device, or Forest as Sap.

Changing Sap into Water requires direct user approval and a review that confirms the selected information is non-sensitive.

Changing Tar Sap into Sap or Water requires the user and must never happen automatically.

### Refined Fluids

Maple's refined Fluids use canonical names tied to their protection grade:

```text
Water-grade Syrup = Light Syrup
Sap-grade Syrup = Syrup
Tar-Sap-grade Syrup = Molasses
```

A Light Syrup is a low-sensitivity refined Fluid.

A Syrup is a protected refined Fluid.

A Molasses is a Maple-bound Tar-Sap-grade refined Fluid for highly private or high-risk local tasks.

Light Syrup, Syrup, and Molasses keep the strongest protection required by their sources.

Maple may feed compatible Light Syrup or Syrup to another authorized Tree as additive Fertilizer.

Molasses may not be absorbed by another Tree.

### Delivery and Absorption Are Separate

Rain and Streams describe how Fluids move.

Watering and Syrup Feeding describe what a sender does.

Washing over, sipping, drinking, and downloading describe how much of a Fluid the receiving Tree absorbs.

These movement and absorption choices do not change the Fluid's protection grade.

A Water message may arrive as Rain or flow through a Stream.

A Sap message may arrive as protected Rain or flow through a Sap Stream.

A Light Syrup or Syrup item may arrive as Rain, flow through a Stream, or be fed directly to a Tree.

Molasses remains local and Maple-bound and may flow only into an approved local Maple action.

Tar Sap may flow only through approved local protected Taps and Streams and must not rain across separate users' Forests.

Sycamore handles approved Fluid routing and connection selection.

Cedar may monitor Fluid intake, Oil eligible Fluids according to their protection grade and policy, regulate absorption, and block unsafe or unauthorized material.

A receiving Tree may absorb only the Fluids allowed by the user, the receiving Tree's permissions, Sycamore's routing decision, and any required Cedar safety decision.

A Tree should preserve provenance linking a Fluid to the Leaf, Flower, Fruit, Log, instruction, preference, Syrup source, or other information from which it was created.

A Tree should explain the Fluid's protection grade, delivery form, absorption mode, and destination when the user asks.

A Grove uses Fluids to pass task inputs, intermediate results, status, approvals, and outputs between participating Trees.

Each Branch Call should carry only the minimum Fluid needed for its step.

A Tree's internal Branch is not itself transferred as a Fluid.

## 🌿 Information Growth Cycle

The Information Growth Cycle explains the difference between information a Tree stores internally, growth a Tree produces, Fluids Trees share, and the ways those Fluids move or become absorbed.

### Internal Information

Internal Information is information that remains part of a Tree's own structure, context, history, or current working state.

Internal Information may include:

- Roots
- Grain Pattern
- Growth Rings
- Logs
- Approved memory
- Fertilizer already absorbed by the Tree
- Tree configuration
- Branch and Twig state
- Local task context
- Health and capability state
- User preferences and routines
- Other authorized information held inside the Tree or local Forest

Internal Information is not automatically a Fluid.

Internal Information remains local until a Tree converts an approved part of it into transferable information.

A Tree should not expose its complete Roots, Grain Pattern, Logs, memories, or internal state merely because the user asks it to share one result.

### Growth Outputs

Growth Outputs are the useful forms a Tree grows after combining Internal Information with authorized inputs.

Growth Outputs include:

- **Leaves** as usable information, observations, evidence, signals, and source material
- **Buds** as possible ideas and early approaches
- **Blossoms** as visible drafts, proposals, and developing work
- **Flowers** as the natural-language term for active Buds and Blossoms
- **Fruit** as completed, accepted, delivered, or finalized work

Growth Outputs are not automatically Fluids.

A Leaf, Flower, or Fruit stays on its Tree until the user or Tree uses it locally, stores it, refines it, or converts its approved information into a Fluid.

### Fluid Conversion

Fluid Conversion is the process of turning approved information from a Leaf, Bud, Blossom, Fruit, Log, memory, preference, instruction, or other source into transferable information.

A Tree does not hand its internal Leaf, Flower, or Fruit directly to another Tree.

A Tree creates a Fluid representation carrying the approved meaning needed by the receiver.

The original Growth Output may remain on the sending Tree after conversion.

The protection grade depends on sensitivity and trust:

- Non-sensitive approved information becomes **Water**.
- Protected information becomes **Sap**.
- Highly private information becomes **Tar Sap** and remains local.
- Refined information created by Maple becomes **Syrup** and also receives a Water, Sap, or Tar Sap protection grade.

A Leaf may become Water because the approved information selected from it is non-sensitive and intended for quick communication.

The user may promote that Water to Sap before sending it or while defining a future rule.

A user asking why a Leaf became Water should receive an explanation such as:

```text
The Leaf remained on your Tree.
Its approved non-sensitive meaning became Water for quick sharing.
Your friend's Forest received the Water rather than your Tree's internal Leaf.
You can promote this Water to Sap when you want stronger protection.
```

A conversion explanation should show:

- The original Growth Output
- The information selected for transfer
- The protection-grade decision
- The resulting Fluid
- Information withheld or summarized
- The receiving Tree, user, or Forest
- The Rain or Stream delivery form
- The Tap and Path
- The selected absorption mode
- Whether the original output remains on the sending Tree

### Fluid, Delivery, and Absorption Distinctions

A Fluid is transferable information.

A protection grade describes sensitivity, trust, and safeguards.

A delivery form describes how the Fluid moves.

An absorption mode describes how much information the receiving Tree keeps and incorporates.

The distinctions are:

- **Water, Sap, and Tar Sap** are Fluid protection grades.
- **Syrup** is a refined Fluid that also carries one of those protection grades.
- **Rain** is a distributed event, update, or notification delivery form.
- **Streams** are sustained flows used for downloads, synchronization, or ongoing sessions.
- **Paths** are routes.
- **Taps** control access to routes.
- **Watering** is the deliberate act of sending Water.
- **Syrup Feeding** is the act of nourishing another Tree with compatible Syrup.
- **Fertilizer** is a separate durable growth package.
- **Washing over, sipping, drinking, and downloading** describe absorption behavior.

The same Water may be Watered by the sender, arrive as Rain, wash over most Trees, and be sipped by the one Tree responsible for the requested action.

The same Sap may flow through an encrypted Stream during a screen-sharing or game session without being permanently stored.

### Washing Over, Sipping, Drinking, and Downloading

**Washing Over** is transient processing.

A Fluid washes over a Tree when the Tree reads or routes the information needed for the current moment without storing the full content or turning it into permanent growth.

The design target is for roughly 99 percent of ordinary pass-through Water and live Stream data to wash over Trees unless the task needs retention.

**Sipping** is minimal absorption.

A Tree sips when it retains only the smallest useful part of a Fluid.

For a calendar reminder, a Tree may sip the reminder title, time, source, and action result without downloading the sender's entire calendar.

**Drinking** is task-scoped absorption.

A Tree drinks when it absorbs enough approved Fluid to complete a task, grow outputs, or make a permitted local change.

**Downloading** is deeper and more durable absorption from a Stream or package.

During a Download, a Tree may absorb much more of the Fluid and attempt to grow Branches, Twigs, Leaves, Flowers, Fruit, Logs, or Growth Rings needed for the downloaded capability or content.

Receiving a Fluid does not automatically mean drinking or downloading it.

A received Fluid may be:

- Washing over the Tree
- Waiting
- Filtered
- Quick Oiled
- Soaked in Oil
- Quarantined
- Rejected
- Expired
- Sipped
- Drunk for one task
- Downloaded for approved durable use

Drinking or downloading does not automatically grant permission to learn preferences, keep permanent memory, execute code, install a Branch, place raw content in Logs, create Syrup, or reshare the Fluid.

Those uses remain separate permissions.

### Growth from Fluids

After sipping, drinking, or downloading an authorized Fluid, a Tree combines the useful information with its own Roots, Grain Pattern, Growth Rings, Logs, Branches, Twigs, task context, and other approved Internal Information.

The Tree may then grow:

- New Leaves
- New Buds
- New Blossoms and Flowers
- New Fruit
- New Branches or Twigs after an approved Download
- New Growth Rings
- New preference or workflow suggestions
- New Syrup when Maple performs the refinement

Useful, relevant, safe Fluids may help a Tree grow more useful outputs.

A large amount of Fluid does not guarantee useful growth.

Cedar and the receiving Tree may regulate intake when Fluids are repetitive, irrelevant, unsafe, conflicting, too large, or likely to overwhelm the Tree.

### Using What a Tree Grows

A user may use Leaves to:

- Inspect evidence and source material
- Answer follow-up questions
- Support a decision
- Grow Buds, Blossoms, or Fruit
- Convert approved information into Water, Sap, or Syrup
- Save useful material for later

A user may use Flowers to:

- Review possible ideas
- Compare approaches
- Continue a draft
- Ask another Tree to collaborate
- Approve a Blossom to become Fruit
- Convert an approved draft into a Fluid for sharing

A user may use Fruit to:

- Complete a task
- Deliver or export finished work
- Save it as Golden Fruit
- Revise or supersede it
- Convert approved information into Water or Sap
- Refine it into Syrup with Maple
- Package useful growth information into Fertilizer
- Share it with another Tree through Rain, a Stream, Watering, or Syrup Feeding

A receiving Tree may use the Fluid temporarily, sip it, drink it, or download it according to permissions and task needs.

The receiving Tree's output is new growth shaped by its own Roots, Grain Pattern, permissions, and context rather than a direct transfer of the sending Tree's internal growth object.

### Information Growth Examples

```text
Maple learns the user's approved favorite-color preference.
→ Maple refines it into protected Syrup.
→ Maple feeds or streams the Syrup to Cherry.
→ Cherry may use the preference without receiving Maple's complete Logs.
```

```text
A friend schedules: "Tell Alex I said happy birthday."
→ The friend's Forest creates Water at the scheduled time.
→ The Water arrives as Rain addressed to Alex's Forest.
→ Alex's Forest routes it to the Trees that should deliver or use it.
→ Most Trees let it wash over them; the responsible Tree sips what it needs.
```

```text
Two friends watch a movie together.
→ Their Trees establish a Stream.
→ Playback, pause, seek, and timing Fluids flow continuously.
→ The information washes over the Trees during the session.
→ The Trees do not remember every frame or song by default.
```

```text
Two friends share screens and game-session details.
→ The Trees establish a Sap Stream.
```

## 💦 Water

Water is the lowest-sensitivity Fluid protection grade.

Water is intended for information that authorized Trees may read and use quickly with minimal content-level restrictions.

Water may still travel through encrypted transport, authenticated Taps, and authorized Streams.

Water's lower protection grade means the information itself is considered non-sensitive within the approved receiving scope.

Water may include:

- Public or non-sensitive messages
- Routine reminders
- Presence and availability signals
- Non-sensitive status updates
- Approved calendar requests containing no protected details
- Shared task progress containing no sensitive user data
- General coordination instructions
- Public links
- Low-risk device or service health signals
- Preplanned greetings such as a happy-birthday message
- Small local automation events

Water is commonly used for quick Tree-to-Tree communication.

Water may arrive as Rain, flow through a Stream, or be deliberately sent through Watering.

Water is not automatically trusted merely because it is easy to read.

Cedar may inspect, filter, Oil, quarantine, rate-limit, or reject Water.

A request delivered as Water does not grant permission to perform a sensitive action.

The receiving user and local Tree permissions still control calendars, reminders, files, applications, devices, accounts, automations, and remote actions.

### Promoting Water to Sap

The user may promote Water to Sap whenever the user wants stronger encryption, narrower access, stricter retention, more deliberate routing, or protection from a less-trusted recipient.

Promotion should preserve provenance and record who changed the protection grade and why.

The user may define standing rules such as:

```text
Treat all Water from User X as Sap.
Treat birthday messages as Water.
Treat device addresses as Sap.
Promote this Water to Sap before sending it.
```

A Tree must not automatically demote Sap into Water merely to improve speed or compatibility.

### Water Retention

Most Water from other Trees should wash over the receiving Forest rather than becoming permanent memory.

A Tree should sip only what it needs for the current task unless the user grants a broader purpose.

For a reminder, the responsible Tree may keep the message, time, sender, result, and required Action Receipt without absorbing the sender's full calendar or unrelated context.

During a Water download, the receiving Tree may drink more deeply and use the information to grow approved Branches, Twigs, Leaves, Flowers, Fruit, Logs, or Growth Rings.

## 🚿 Watering

Watering is the deliberate authorized act of sending Water to a Tree, group of Trees, device, user, or Forest.

A user may Water one specific Tree or address the receiving Forest as a whole.

When Water is addressed to a Forest, the receiving Forest may automatically route the message to the Trees needed to deliver it or complete the requested low-risk task.

The sender does not need to know whether Cherry, Maple, Sycamore, or another Tree is the best local recipient.

Watering may include:

- Sending a friend a reminder
- Scheduling a future message
- Telling a friend's Forest, "Treat today like Alex's birthday"
- Telling a friend's Forest, "Tell Alex I said happy birthday"
- Sharing a public event
- Sending a non-sensitive task update
- Asking whether a friend or Tree is available
- Triggering a low-risk automation inside the user's own Forest
- Sharing the minimum coordination information needed by several local Trees

A Watering event may come directly from the user, another Tree, the user's Soil, a local automation, or another authorized Forest component.

Watering does not give the sender direct control over the receiving Forest.

The receiving Forest decides which local Trees should see, sip, drink, route, delay, reject, or act on the Water.

The receiving Tree performs any approved action locally on its own Soil.

Watering must respect:

- The receiving user's permissions
- The receiving Tree's allowed Branches
- Sycamore's relationship and session rules
- Cedar's Water-intake policy
- Calendar, reminder, application, and account-specific authorization
- Expiration, revocation, and rate limits

A Watering request may be delivered successfully without being stored or acted upon.

The receiving Forest may reject, delay, filter, quarantine, request confirmation, let the Water wash over unrelated Trees, or allow the responsible Tree to sip what it needs.

Watering is described from the sender's point of view.

When the delivery uses the Rain pattern, the receiving Forest experiences it as Rain.

A user asking how to get Water to a friend should be shown the available Watering targets, Rain options, Streams, Taps, Sycamore connection methods, expected Cedar treatment, receiving permissions, and whether Fertilizer or Syrup would better fit the desired long-term result.

## 🌧️ Rain

Rain is a distributed delivery form for small events, updates, notices, requests, and coordination information.

Rain is not a Fluid protection grade.

Rain carries a Fluid.

Water is the most common Fluid carried as Rain because Rain is especially useful for quick, low-sensitivity updates and notifications.

Authorized Sap or Syrup may also arrive as Rain when their stronger protection, routing, and permission requirements remain intact.

Tar Sap must not rain across separate users' Forests.

Rain may come from:

- Another user's Forest
- The user's own local Forest
- A Tree
- The user's Soil
- A scheduled event
- A local automation
- The Forest Canopy
- An approved application or service

Rain may target:

- One Tree
- Several selected Trees
- All eligible Trees in one Forest
- A user without naming a specific Tree
- Several authorized Forests
- A selected project, device, or workflow scope

Rain is useful when the sender wants the receiving Forest to distribute an update without addressing every Tree individually.

Example:

```text
Tell Alex I said happy birthday.
→ Water is created at the scheduled time.
→ The Water arrives as Rain addressed to Alex's Forest.
→ Alex's Forest identifies the Trees that should deliver or use it.
→ Unrelated Trees let it wash over them without storing it.
```

Local Rain may also coordinate the user's own Trees.

Example:

```text
A scheduled automation begins.
→ One Tree creates the required event Fluid.
→ Rain distributes the minimum information to the Trees needed for the task.
→ Each Tree sips only the part needed for its role.
```

Rain should normally carry small or bounded updates rather than a large continuous flow.

A sustained or high-volume exchange should normally establish a Stream instead of sending repeated Rain events.

Rain may be:

- Allowed to wash over most Trees
- Sipped by the Tree responsible for an action
- Drunk when the task requires more context
- Rejected, delayed, Oiled, Soaked, filtered, or quarantined

Rain does not automatically become memory, Logs, preferences, Leaves, Flowers, Fruit, Syrup, or Fertilizer.

The Fluid carried by Rain keeps its own Water, Sap, or Tar Sap protection grade.

Syrup carried as Rain keeps its assigned protection grade.

## 🟤 Sap

Sap is the protected Fluid grade for sensitive information that authorized Trees or Forests need for an approved task.

Sap contains the same general kind of transferable information as Water, but Sap uses stronger protection because the information is more sensitive, the recipient is less trusted, or the user wants narrower access and retention.

Water may become Sap whenever the user promotes it.

Sap may include:

- IP addresses, private network addresses, route details, and connection metadata
- Screen-sharing state, display selection, resolution, refresh rate, and viewed status
- Computer status, including online state, sleep state, battery state, resource pressure, and approved telemetry
- Current software, game, media player, or shared application
- Active-window or application state when approved
- Game title, version, platform, lobby, server, session, and compatibility information
- Game invitations, join tokens, session credentials, and temporary capabilities
- Installed DLC, approved mods, hashes, map versions, and content manifests
- Player presence, party state, role, team, and approved profile information
- Controller, input, and accessibility details
- Screen-stream resolution, frame rate, bitrate, codec, latency, jitter, packet loss, and relay state
- Voice, media, microphone, speaker, subtitle, language, and audio-track state
- Media title, queue, playback position, pause state, speed, and synchronization timestamp
- Shared task state
- Approved desktop-action parameters
- Private collaboration data
- File-transfer keys, manifests, hashes, chunks, and resume state
- Temporary permissions and session-scoped authorization
- Protected reminders or calendar details
- Sensitive representations of Leaves, Buds, Blossoms, Fruit, preferences, or Syrup

Sap must not include passwords, permanent private keys, recovery codes, or other Tar Sap merely because a game, media session, or shared task requests them.

Sap is sticky and hard to get through.

Sap should use end-to-end encryption, verified endpoints, integrity checks, task-scoped permissions, and controlled retention.

Sap should not be intentionally throttled.

The required encryption, verification, Cedar treatment, decryption, interpretation, and permission checks may create unavoidable overhead and lower effective transfer speed compared with equivalent Water.

Sap may arrive as protected Rain for a small bounded event.

Sap may flow through a Sap Stream for screen sharing, remote control, synchronized media, game sessions, downloads, and other sustained protected exchanges.

The Canopy and relay infrastructure may transport Sap without reading its contents.

Sycamore may route Sap but must not expose decrypted Sap outside the approved scope.

Most Sap received from another Tree should wash over or be used temporarily unless the task requires deeper absorption.

A receiving Tree must not automatically turn Sap into permanent memory, training material, Logs, Leaves, Buds, Blossoms, Fruit, preferences, or Syrup unless the user and originating permissions allow that use.

Sap should expire, be revoked, or become unreadable when its session, task, or permission ends.

Sap becomes Tar Sap when the user marks it local-only and unavailable to Trees outside the user's Forest.

Maple may create Sap-grade Syrup from protected information.

Sap may enter or leave an approved Path through a Sap Tap.

`Lock Sap Tap` stops new protected Sap flow through the selected scope while preserving queued Sap safely.

## 🍁 Syrup

Syrup is Maple's refined Fluid family.

The Syrup family contains three canonical forms:

```text
Light Syrup = Water-grade Syrup
Syrup = Sap-grade Syrup
Molasses = Tar-Sap-grade Syrup
```

A Light Syrup is an easily readable, low-sensitivity refined Fluid.

A Syrup is a protected refined Fluid containing ordinary sensitive, personalized, or task-scoped information.

A Molasses is a highly protected Maple-bound refined Fluid for extremely sensitive, deeply personal, or high-risk local tasks.

Maple selects the form according to the strongest protection required by the source information and intended action.

The user may ask Maple to increase protection:

```text
Maple, thicken this Light Syrup into Syrup.
Maple, thicken this Syrup into Molasses.
```

Reducing protection requires direct user approval and a new classification review.

Light Syrup, Syrup, and Molasses may carry a useful result, pattern, configuration, idea, workflow, tool, template, task plan, preference, or compatible growth nutrient.

Each refined Fluid should record:

- Its canonical form
- Its source categories
- Its protection level
- Its Syrup Bucket state
- Its intended user, Tree, application, or action
- Its required permissions
- Its allowed destinations
- Its expiration or review time
- Its undo or disable method when applicable
- Its provenance and Growth Ring references

Maple should extract safe patterns, structures, preferences, and useful rules rather than copying private source material when the task does not need the raw content.

### Syrup Is a Fertilizer Fluid

Every Light Syrup, Syrup, and Molasses item is Fertilizer from the moment Maple creates it.

Syrup remains a Fluid at the same time.

`Fluid` describes transfer and protection behavior.

`Fertilizer` describes the item's ability to nourish compatible growth.

Syrup Feeding is the act of delivering a Syrup-family Fertilizer Fluid so a Tree may absorb compatible growth nutrients.

Syrup Feeding follows the same additive and modifying boundary as Fertilizing.

Syrup Feeding may:

- Add approved preferences
- Add a reusable workflow
- Add a safe template
- Add a compatible Branch or Twig configuration
- Add a known task pattern
- Add approved Growth Ring information
- Modify a specific supported setting
- Extend a Grain Pattern
- Help a Tree understand the user's preferred style or workflow

Syrup Feeding must not:

- Delete existing information
- Delete, replace, or overwrite files
- Replace the receiving Tree's identity
- Replace the receiving Tree's Roots
- Replace the receiving Tree's complete Grain Pattern
- Remove security controls
- Expand permissions silently
- Turn the receiving Tree into a wholesale copy of Maple or another Tree

A receiving Tree absorbs only the Syrup nutrients compatible with its Roots, role, permissions, Branches, Twigs, Soil, and current health.

A receiving Tree must report nutrients it accepted, modified, skipped, rejected, or quarantined.

A Syrup-family item is Fertilizer but does not automatically become a Fertilizer File.

Light Syrup and Syrup may nourish authorized receiving Trees.

Molasses nourishes Maple locally because Molasses remains Maple-bound.

Another Tree may receive only a separately created minimum safe Light Syrup or Syrup derivative from a Molasses-backed task.

Maple or the user may separately package approved Syrup nutrients into a Fertilizer file.

### Automatic Syrup Feeding

Automatic Syrup Feeding is Maple's per-Tree option to feed approved Syrup to a selected Tree without asking for every compatible item.

Automatic Syrup Feeding is off by default for every Tree.

The user may enable it separately for:

- One Tree
- One Sapling
- One Sprout
- One Branch or Twig
- One project
- One Syrup category
- One source category
- One temporary session

A per-Tree Automatic Syrup Feeding rule should define:

- The receiving Tree
- Allowed Syrup categories
- Allowed source categories
- Allowed sensitivity levels
- Whether preference Syrup is allowed
- Whether workflow Syrup is allowed
- Whether code, media, note, or Forest-management Syrup is allowed
- Whether the Tree may modify existing approved values
- Whether Maple must preview each feeding
- A maximum frequency or rate
- An expiration or review date

Automatic Syrup Feeding must not include Tar Sap-derived Syrup unless the user grants separate, purpose-specific, local-only permission for that exact receiving Tree and scope.

Automatic Syrup Feeding must stop when the receiving Tree becomes Wounded, Quarantined, Unknown, or incompatible with the Syrup.

### Syrup Approval

A Syrup Approval is the user's confirmation that Maple may create, save, activate, feed, or share a specific Syrup item.

A Syrup Approval should be separate when the item:

## 🟫 Molasses

A Molasses is Tar-Sap-grade Syrup created by Maple for a highly sensitive, high-risk, or deeply personal local task.

A Molasses is a Fluid.

A Molasses is Maple-bound.

Maple-bound means:

- Only the authorized local Maple instance may decrypt and interpret the Molasses contents
- Another Tree may not absorb, inspect, search, index, summarize, embed, learn from, log the contents of, or independently act on Molasses
- Another Tree may request Maple's help through a minimal Water or Sap request
- Maple may return only the minimum safe result required by the requesting Tree or user
- The Spirit, Cedar, and protected Forest code may enforce the boundary without reading the Molasses contents
- Sycamore may not route Molasses outside the user's local Forest
- A remote model or external service may not receive Molasses

A Molasses may be created from:

- Tar Sap
- A high-risk task involving identity, money, credentials, private records, or exact personal details
- A user instruction requiring Maple to combine several protected sources
- A Syrup the user deliberately thickens into Molasses
- Another approved local source requiring Tar-Sap-grade protection

A Molasses should contain the minimum task-scoped information needed.

Permanent passwords, private keys, recovery codes, and long-term authentication secrets remain Tar Sap in protected stores.

Molasses should use short-lived task instructions, protected references, temporary capabilities, and the minimum approved context instead of copying permanent secrets into Maple.

### Molasses Assistance

Another Tree may not absorb Molasses.

Another Tree may ask Maple to assist with a Molasses-backed task.

Maple remains the only interpreter and actor for the Molasses portion.

The requesting Tree may receive only:

- A safe status
- A success or failure result
- A user-approved Action Receipt
- A minimum Water or Sap summary
- A non-sensitive follow-up instruction

### High-Risk Molasses Actions

A high-risk Molasses action is an action involving money, identity, credentials, private records, legal commitments, irreversible changes, or similarly sensitive consequences.

For a high-risk action, Maple must:

1. Confirm the exact user instruction.
2. Confirm the account, target, recipient, amount, or object involved.
3. Show a final preview.
4. Require direct user confirmation immediately before submission.
5. Use the service's own authentication and security checks.
6. Create an Action Receipt.
7. Avoid retaining credentials or unnecessary sensitive contents afterward.
8. Expire temporary capabilities when the task ends.

Maple must not perform a financial transfer, purchase, account change, or comparable high-risk action solely because another Tree requested it.

Maple must not make high-risk Molasses actions unattended by default.

An example is:

```text
User: Maple, open my bank and prepare a $100 transfer to my saved recipient.

Maple:
- opens the approved local banking session
- uses protected local credential references
- prepares the transfer
- shows the recipient, amount, fees, and final effect
- waits for the user's direct confirmation
- submits only after confirmation
- records a protected Action Receipt
```

### Molasses Commands

```text
Maple, show which Trees requested help with this Molasses.
```

Cedar may strengthen the surrounding Forest's protection but does not become the reader, owner, or interpreter of Molasses.

A user may use local Molasses tasks without Cedar when Maple's protected local runtime is healthy and the required confirmations are satisfied.

## 🪣 Syrup Bucket

A Syrup Bucket is Maple's user-visible workspace for potential ideas, active refinements, held concepts, generated Syrup, and Syrup waiting for a decision.

A Syrup Bucket allows the user to see what Maple is digesting, what Maple is making, and why Maple is holding an item.

A Syrup Bucket item may have one of these states:

- **Digesting** — Maple is reviewing approved sources and identifying useful patterns
- **Refining** — Maple is actively converting an idea or pattern into Syrup
- **Ready** — Maple has a Syrup draft or result ready for review
- **Holding** — Maple believes the idea may be useful but is waiting for context, permission, compatibility, or a better time
- **Waiting for Permission** — Maple requires a specific permission before continuing
- **Blocked** — Cedar, the Spirit, a protected Root, a permission rule, or the user prevents Maple from continuing
- **Feeding** — Maple is feeding approved Syrup to a Tree
- **Fed** — Maple completed an approved Syrup Feeding
- **Discarded** — The item was removed from the Syrup Bucket and moved into Leaf Litter

A Syrup Bucket entry should show:

- A safe title
- Its source category and safe provenance reference
- What Maple thinks the idea could become
- What Maple is currently generating
- Why Maple is holding, blocking, or delaying it
- Its current protection level
- The permissions Maple has and still needs
- Its intended user, Tree, project, application, or workflow
- Whether it may act as Fertilizer
- Whether it is eligible for automatic Syrup Feeding
- Whether it was revived from Leaf Litter
- Its creation, update, review, and expiration times
- Available actions

The user may open, filter, search, sort, pause, resume, approve, feed, archive, or remove Syrup Bucket items.

### Removing Syrup Bucket Items

Removing a Syrup Bucket item moves the Bucket entry and Maple-generated working material into Leaf Litter.

Removing a Syrup Bucket item must not move or delete the original source note, file, Fruit, Leaf, Log, or Tar Sap unless the user separately requests that action.

Maple must mark the discarded item:

```yaml
maple_ignore: true
discarded_from: "syrup-bucket"
discarded_by: "<USER-ID>"
discarded_at: "<TIMESTAMP>"
```

An ordinarily ignored Syrup item must be excluded from Maple's normal suggestions, automatic Syrup generation, preference learning, and Tree feeding.

Maple may retain the smallest safe reason that the user discarded the item so Maple does not repeatedly recreate the same unwanted idea.

### Reabsorbing Discarded Syrup

Reabsorb Discarded Syrup is Maple's option to occasionally reconsider previously discarded Syrup ideas and projects.

Reabsorb Discarded Syrup is off by default.

When enabled, Maple may temporarily ignore an ordinary `maple_ignore` mark during a scheduled revival review.

A revival review may:

- Reconsider whether the discarded idea is useful under new circumstances
- Compare the item with new goals, projects, tools, preferences, or Forest capabilities
- Return a safe candidate to the Syrup Bucket
- Mark the candidate as **Revived**
- Explain why Maple believes the item deserves another look

A revival review must not automatically activate, execute, feed, share, or modify anything.

The user may set the **Ignore Override Frequency** to:

- Once
- Weekly
- Monthly
- Quarterly
- A custom interval
- Manual only

The Ignore Override Frequency controls how often Maple may temporarily overlook ordinary ignore marks during a revival review.

### Never Reabsorb

Never Reabsorb is a permanent user-controlled mark that prevents an item from returning to Maple's Syrup Bucket during automatic or manual revival scans.

A Never Reabsorb item is double-marked:

```yaml
maple_ignore: true
never_reabsorb: true
```

Maple must honor `never_reabsorb: true` even when Reabsorb Discarded Syrup is enabled and Maple is temporarily ignoring ordinary ignore marks.

Only the user may remove a Never Reabsorb mark.

A Never Reabsorb item may remain in Leaf Litter for evidence, history, or later manual deletion according to Leaf Litter policy.

### Forest Commands

```text
Maple, open your Syrup Bucket.
Maple, show what you are digesting.
Maple, show what you are generating.
Maple, explain why you are holding this.
Maple, remove this from your Syrup Bucket.
Maple, discard this Syrup and ignore it.
Maple, mark this Never Reabsorb.
Maple, unmark this Never Reabsorb.
Maple, enable Reabsorb Discarded Syrup.
Maple, set Ignore Override Frequency to monthly.
Maple, run a Syrup revival review now.
Maple, return this discarded idea to your Syrup Bucket.
```

A Cedar-cleared plugin or mod idea may appear in Maple's Syrup Bucket as a potential workflow improvement.

A plugin or mod candidate must retain its Cedar review state, target application, compatible version range, source, requested permissions, and rollback information while it remains in the Syrup Bucket.

A discarded plugin or mod suggestion follows the normal Maple Ignore and Never Reabsorb rules.

The Syrup Bucket supplies Maple's Syrup Tap.

The Syrup Bucket stores potential and active Syrup.

The Syrup Tap controls what may leave the Bucket and where it may go.

`What's coming through the Tap?` shows active and queued Syrup flow.

`What's blocking the Tap?` shows permission, Cedar, Spirit, Sycamore, health, compatibility, classification, destination, or user blocks.

The Syrup Bucket may contain Light Syrup, Syrup, and Molasses.

A Molasses entry reveals only safe metadata unless the user opens it through Maple with the required local authorization.

Another Tree may not preview or absorb Molasses from the Syrup Bucket.

## ⚫ Tar Sap

Tar Sap is highly private user data that no Tree outside the user's Forest may access or absorb.

Tar Sap may include:

- Passwords
- Authentication secrets
- Private keys
- Recovery codes
- Personal identity details
- Private notes
- Protected journals
- Financial details
- Medical details
- Exact private locations
- Information the user explicitly marks as never shareable outside the local Forest

Tar Sap receives the Forest's strongest default protection.

A Tree inside the user's Forest may access Tar Sap only when the user explicitly allows that Tree and that specific purpose.

A Tree outside the user's Forest may not:

- Read Tar Sap
- Decrypt Tar Sap
- Search Tar Sap
- Index Tar Sap
- Summarize Tar Sap
- Embed Tar Sap
- Learn from Tar Sap
- Inspect Tar Sap
- Preview Tar Sap
- Store Tar Sap
- Transfer Tar Sap onward
- Absorb Tar Sap as memory, Leaves, Buds, Blossoms, Fruit, Logs, context, or training material

A Sycamore Tree may enforce the Tar Sap boundary, but a Sycamore Tree may not read or decrypt Tar Sap.

A Cedar Tree may verify that Tar Sap protections are active, but a Cedar Tree may not inspect protected Tar Sap contents unless the user grants local, purpose-specific access.

Tar Sap must remain:

- Bound to the user's local Forest
- Encrypted at rest
- Protected with keys unavailable outside the user's Forest
- Excluded from cross-Forest Streams
- Excluded from The Forest Canopy
- Excluded from relays, gateways, and Watering
- Excluded from external search, indexing, embeddings, telemetry, and automatic learning
- Hidden from previews and generated summaries
- Represented in Logs only by the minimum non-sensitive policy event needed to prove that access was blocked

A foreign Tree that receives Tar Sap ciphertext by error or attack must remain unable to decrypt or absorb it.

A foreign Forest must reject Tar Sap before it reaches its Trees.

A Tree must fail closed when Tar Sap identity, origin, destination, or permission cannot be verified.

Only the user may reclassify Tar Sap as Sap or Water.

### Forest Commands

```text
Mark this as Tar Sap.
Show Tar Sap protections.
Keep this Tar Sap inside my Forest.
Allow this local Tree to use this Tar Sap once.
Cancel Tar Sap access.
Remove this from Tar Sap.
```

`Remove this from Tar Sap.` requires direct user confirmation and must show which Trees, tools, Paths, devices, users, or services would gain access after reclassification.

Tar Sap may be accessed locally only through an authorized Tar Tap.

`Lock Tar Tap` is the emergency command for closing new Tar Sap access without moving or reclassifying the Tar Sap.

Tar Sap is the highest Fluid protection grade.

Tar Sap may use only approved local protected Taps and Streams inside the user's Forest.

Tar Sap must not use cross-Forest Rain, Watering, relays, gateways, or Streams.

Tar-Sap-grade Syrup remains subject to the same local-only boundary.

Maple may refine approved Tar Sap into task-scoped Molasses.

Molasses does not replace the original Tar Sap store.

Permanent credentials and secrets remain Tar Sap and should be referenced by Molasses rather than copied into it.

- Corrections
- Priorities
- Time spent using a Branch
- Deliberate focus

Sunlight does not directly rewrite Roots. It helps the Tree decide which healthy Branches, Buds, and Blossoms deserve attention.

### Notes as Sunlight

Obsidian, notes applications, notebooks, journals, project notes, planning documents, and other approved written records count as Sunlight when the user authorizes a Tree to use them.

Notes can be among the largest contributors to a Tree growing:

- More useful Leaves
- Deeper and more informative Growth Rings
- A Grain Pattern that better reflects the user's preferences, goals, routines, communication style, and desired ways of working

Notes provide sustained Sunlight because they may contain long-term goals, repeated corrections, decisions, preferences, project history, personal organization patterns, and explanations that are not present in a single prompt.

A Tree may convert approved note information into Leaves and use those Leaves in its Active Foliage.

The Tree may also grow Sturdy Leaves, preferences, workflows, Growth Rings, or Grain Pattern adjustments when the user's instructions and permissions support that growth.

Notes do not automatically become permanent memory merely because they count as Sunlight.

Leaf states, source permissions, scope, protection levels, preference-learning controls, and user-created separation boundaries still apply.

### Notes Access

Sunlight may be directed into a notes application so a Tree can use approved notes as active context, focus, or guidance.

This access must be permission-based.

Obsidian is the first named notes application supported by this definition.

Notes access is **inactive by default**. A Tree must not open, read, search, index, summarize, write, reorganize, or monitor notes until the user grants explicit permission and defines the allowed scope.

Permission may be limited by:

- Notes application
- Vault
- Folder
- File
- Read-only or read-and-write access
- User profile
- Tree
- Branch
- Session
- Time period
- Purpose

The user may revoke access at any time.

### Future AI Note Tools

Future AI note tools may be added here after review and approval.

```yaml
future_ai_note_tools:
  - name: "<TOOL-NAME>"
    status: "inactive"
    permission_scope: "none"
    approved: false
```

A future AI note tool remains inactive until it is deliberately approved, configured, and granted permission.

Maple may use approved Sunlight, such as personal notes, goals, priorities, project plans, and user attention, to improve workflows and create Syrup.

Sunlight access remains inactive by default and must be granted by the user for a specific Tree, source, folder, notebook, project, or task.

Maple should use Sunlight to identify useful patterns and priorities without copying private source text into outputs unless the user requests it.

Maple's Notes and Knowledge Branch may receive Sunlight from Obsidian and other approved note applications.

Sunlight permissions may be limited by vault, folder, note, tag, project, connector, access mode, or temporary session.

Maple may use approved Sunlight to learn context-specific preferences, goals, priorities, organization patterns, creative styles, and workflow preferences.

Maple should record the useful preference or pattern rather than unnecessary raw note content whenever possible.

## 🚷 Hackers

Hackers are active human attackers, unauthorized operators, or malicious insiders attempting to harm, steal from, manipulate, surveil, deceive, or gain unauthorized control over the Forest.

Hackers are known for hacking Trees and Soil.

When Hackers compromise Soil, they may damage or disrupt the servers, devices, storage, networks, services, applications, and other resources that support Trees and the wider Forest.

Hacker activity may include:

- Credential theft
- Social engineering
- Unauthorized access
- Account takeover
- Data theft
- Sabotage
- Malicious configuration changes
- Abuse of connected services
- Attempts to plant harmful Seeds
- Attempts to disable alerts or Logs
- Manipulation of Trees or users
- Attacks against email, Discord, repositories, servers, or other connected services

### Forest Protections Against Hackers

The Forest includes several layers that help protect against Hackers:

- **Cedar** provides broader Forest monitoring, detection, quarantine, containment, evidence preservation, and recovery support.
- **Tar Sap and Molasses protections** keep the most sensitive information local, tightly scoped, and inaccessible to unauthorized Trees or remote systems.
- **Sycamore** protects connections through identity, trust, permissions, Taps, session controls, route restrictions, and revocation.
- **Maple Local Safeguards** protect entrusted personal data through local-first processing, protected references, confirmations, quarantine, and Action Receipts.
- **Propagation, the Seed Vault, the Log Cabin, Milling, and Replanting** help restore healthy Trees and Forest services after damage.

The Forest should defend against Hackers through prevention, detection, access control, alerting, containment, evidence preservation, and recovery.

The Forest must not retaliate, hack back, dox, or pursue Hackers outside authorized defensive and legal processes.

## 🦠 Mold

Mold is harmful, malicious, compromised, or unwanted software that infects, tracks, damages, deceives, steals from, or weakens the Forest.

Mold may include:

- Viruses
- Malware
- Spyware
- Trackers
- Ransomware
- Worms
- Rootkits
- Credential stealers
- Malicious scripts
- Compromised plugins
- Poisoned model or package files
- Harmful browser extensions
- Corrupted or tampered Seeds
- Unauthorized persistence
- Software that disables health checks or Logs

Mold is different from Leaf Litter.

- **Mold** is active harmful software or code.
- **Leaf Litter** is inactive discarded, incomplete, deprecated, or decaying material.

Mold should be detected, isolated, documented, and removed through approved defensive procedures.

### Forest Protections Against Mold

The Forest includes several layers that help protect against Mold:

- **Cedar Oil, Soak, quarantine, and containment** help inspect suspicious material, isolate threats, and prevent unsafe components from spreading.
- **Verified Seeds, hashes, signatures, and the Seed Vault** help Trees use trusted packages and rebuild from known healthy sources.
- **Maple Local Safeguards** hold unknown plugins, mods, scripts, downloads, and executables for local review before activation.
- **Sycamore Taps, route controls, session revocation, and Cutting** help stop Mold from spreading through connections and Streams.
- **Tar Sap and Molasses boundaries** reduce the information a compromised component can reach.
- **Propagation, the Log Cabin, Felling, Milling, and Replanting** support recovery when a Tree or part of the Forest cannot be safely repaired in place.

These protections should work together.

Cedar provides the broadest Forest-focused defense, while Trees such as Maple and Sycamore retain their own local protections and boundaries.


Recovery may use clean Seeds from the Seed Vault and verified Logs from the Log Cabin.

### Forest Commands

```text
Check for Mold.
```

Inspect authorized systems, Trees, Seeds, plugins, and services for indicators of harmful software.

```text
Report Hacker activity.
```

Create an incident alert with the evidence, affected Paths, and recommended defensive actions.

```text
Quarantine this Mold.
```

Isolate the suspected harmful software or component under an approved containment policy.

```text
Cedar, guard this service.
```

Enable Cedar's approved monitoring and defensive controls for a connected email account, Discord server, repository, or other authorized service.

## ✂️ Pruning

Pruning is targeted maintenance that removes unhealthy, unsafe, redundant, outdated, or unnecessary growth while preserving the Tree and its intended shape.

Pruning may apply to:

- Files
- Notes
- Memories
- Branches
- Plugins
- Models
- Dependencies
- Duplicate Leaves
- Old Blossoms
- Unused features

Pruned material may move to Leaf Litter or the Seed Vault depending on whether it is discarded or intentionally preserved.

Pruning should not silently change a Tree's Roots, Grain Pattern, or identity.


## 🪓 Cutting

Cutting is the Forest's general detachment and isolation mechanism.

A Cut severs a selected connection so damage cannot continue spreading through the Forest.

A Cut may detach:

- A Branch
- A newly attached Root
- A Root connection
- A Stream
- A plugin
- A model
- A Seed
- A remote extension
- A compromised account connection
- A service integration
- A Sapling
- A Tree from the Canopy
- A Tree from part or all of the Forest

Cutting is not automatically destruction.

The separated material may remain intact in quarantine so Cedar or the user can inspect it safely.

### Root Cutting

A Root connection may be cut when it is untrusted, compromised, newly attached without approval, or spreading damage.

Cutting a Root connection severs the Tree's connection to that Root or dependency. It does not silently erase the canonical Root record.

Changing or permanently removing an approved foundational Root remains a governed action.

### Controlled Cut

A Controlled Cut is a planned detachment performed during maintenance, migration, testing, or reconfiguration.

### Emergency Cut

An Emergency Cut is an immediate defensive detachment used to stop active or suspected harm.

An Emergency Cut should:

- Stop affected Streams
- Revoke related access and credentials when authorized
- Preserve evidence when doing so is safe
- Record the exact Path that was severed
- Alert the user, Cedar, and the Canopy
- Check nearby Trees, Saplings, Roots, and Branches for damage
- Prefer reversible isolation before destruction
- Require user approval or a pre-authorized emergency policy

### Forest Commands

```text
Cut this Branch.
Cut this Root connection.
Cut this Stream.
Cut this Tree from the Canopy.
Emergency cut this connection.
Cedar, make the cut.
```

## ⚙️ Wood Chipper

A Wood Chipper, or Chipper for short, is the Forest's destructive sanitization and disposal tool.

The Chipper takes material that has already been Cut or quarantined and breaks it into tiny non-operational pieces so it cannot continue harming the Forest.

The Chipper may process:

- Mold
- Malicious plugins
- Compromised Seeds
- Infected packages
- Dangerous scripts
- Corrupted Branches
- Untrusted model files
- Harmful configuration fragments
- Material from a felled Tree

The Chipper should operate inside a tightly isolated environment.

Its goals are:

1. Make the dangerous material non-executable and harmless.
2. Preserve as much safe information about the attack as possible.
3. Protect the rest of the Forest even when complete analysis is impossible.

Cedar may attempt to reconstruct the incident from inert fragments such as:

- Hashes
- Metadata
- Strings
- Timestamps
- File relationships
- Indicators of compromise
- Package identities
- Paths
- Logs
- Non-executable fragments

Cedar must not reactivate dangerous material merely to understand it.

When safe reconstruction is not possible, protection of the Forest takes priority over complete analysis.

### Forest Commands

```text
Send this to the Chipper.
Cedar, chip this safely.
Chipper, preserve what can be logged.
```

## 🌲 Felling

Felling is the complete shutdown and detachment of an entire Tree.

A Tree may be felled when it is:

- Fully infected
- Persistently compromised
- Structurally damaged
- No longer trustworthy
- Causing widespread harm
- Impossible to repair safely while active

Felling should:

- Stop the Tree
- Sever its active Streams
- Remove its access to sensitive Soil and services
- Notify the user and Canopy
- Preserve safe Logs and evidence
- Record the reason for the action
- Prepare for Milling, Chipping, repair, or replacement

Cedar may recommend Felling at any time.

Cedar may Fell a Tree automatically only under a pre-authorized emergency policy or an enabled Propagated Recovery policy. Otherwise, the user approves the action.

### Forest Commands

```text
Fell this Tree.
Chop this Tree down.
Cedar, fell the infected Tree.
```

## 🪚 Milling

Milling is the controlled dismantling of a felled Tree into smaller inspectable parts.

Milling is used to:

- Separate healthy components from infected ones
- Recover safe Logs
- Identify where Mold entered
- Preserve useful evidence
- Salvage verified Seeds, configurations, or memories
- Prepare dangerous parts for the Chipper
- Learn how to protect replacement Trees
- Recover post-Propagation data that may be safe enough to convert into supplemental Fertilizer for a replacement Sprout

Milling should identify Sturdy Leaves before ordinary salvage.

Sturdy Leaves receive high-priority recovery and preservation attempts.

Milling should preserve verified-safe contents or the smallest safe combination of protected references, integrity hashes, provenance, protection state, scope, recovery instructions, user ownership, and related Growth Rings.

A Sturdy Leaf that cannot yet be verified remains quarantined and marked for recovery review rather than ordinary disposal.

Sturdy priority does not bypass Mold checks, Cedar Oil, classification, permissions, or compatibility review.

A user-marked Sturdy Leaf should not be sent to the Chipper without direct user approval unless an approved emergency destruction rule is needed for an immediate unavoidable threat.

Milling must occur in quarantine.

No milled component returns to active use until it has been verified, approved, assigned a safe Path, and passed through any required Cedar Oil treatment.

### Forest Commands

```text
Mill this Tree for evidence.
Cedar, separate the healthy wood.
Send the infected pieces to the Chipper.
```

## 🌱 Replanting

Replanting is the replacement of a felled, removed, or unrecoverable Tree with a clean Tree or Sapling.

Replanting should use:

- A verified Seed or Propagation file, including an eligible pre-Watering Propagation
- A trusted package from the Seed Vault
- Clean configuration
- Approved Logs and memories
- Newly issued credentials
- Verified Roots and Root connections
- A health check before joining the Canopy

A replacement Tree keeps only the identity, Logs, memories, permissions, and components that have been deliberately approved for restoration.

The infected Tree must not be copied wholesale into the replacement.

A replanted Sprout may be Fertilized with the separate Fertilizer paired to its Propagation.

A replanted Sprout may also receive supplemental Fertilizer made from data recovered through Milling and Cedar Oil.

Fertilizing must add or modify approved values without deleting or replacing existing information or files.

The Sprout must receive Fertilizer in controlled stages with health checks between stages.

### Forest Commands

```text
Replant this Tree.
Plant a clean Seed in this Soil.
Replace this Tree with a Sapling.
Cedar, restore from the Seed Vault.
```


# Paths and Walking

The Local Forest Recovery System performs Replanting for every Tree when the complete Forest is installed.

The Local Forest Recovery System performs Cedar's Replanting independently because a damaged Cedar must not supervise its own replacement.

Cherry's standalone Replant Cedar function may perform user-approved Cedar Replanting only when the complete Forest is absent.

After the Forest is installed, Cherry may provide a candidate Cedar Propagation after normal recovery fails, but the Local Forest Recovery System must verify and use that candidate.


Verified Sturdy Leaves should be restored before ordinary default Leaves when scope and protection permit. Loose Leaves are not normally restored unless still required by an active recovery task or explicitly requested by the user.

## Path

A Path is a resolvable reference or relationship leading from one Forest object to another.

Use:

```text
Path to ADR-0004
```

instead of:

```text
See ADR-0004
```

Paths must remain technically resolvable through the PDC-ID, UUID, canonical path, and relationship indexes.

## Walking Down a Path

Walking down a Path is the act of following its linked records, decisions, Roots, dependencies, Logs, or related objects in an understandable order.

```text
Walk me down the Path to ADR-0004.
```

Explain the important steps leading to ADR-0004 and the records connected to it.

```text
Walk this Path deeper.
```

Continue into the next meaningful layer of dependencies or Roots.

```text
Where does this Path lead?
```

Show the likely downstream records, systems, consequences, or Fruit connected to the current Path.

```text
Where does this Path split?
```

Show alternative decisions, competing approaches, or Branches.

A guided walk should summarize each stop and provide its technical identifier.
